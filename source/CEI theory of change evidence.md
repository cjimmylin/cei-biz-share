# Labs court faith leaders; benchmarks remain unasked

As of October 27, 2026, public evidence supports half of the Center for Ethical Intelligence's theory of change and leaves the other half unproven. The supported half is demand. Anthropic, led personally by co-founder Chris Olah, has spent a year bringing clergy and scholars from **more than 15 religious and cross-cultural groups** into private seminars, and Olah told the Vatican that labs need "informed critics who will tell the labs when we are failing" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/widening-conversation-ai); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/chris-olah-pope-leo-encyclical)). Faith communities have published AI guidance at a fast pace, and pastors say what they want from AI: **60% want it to lay out the major interpretations without choosing one** ([Religion Unplugged](https://religionunplugged.com/news/2026/8/31/pastors-wary-of-ai-interpreting-scripture-but-still-use-technology-study-finds)). The unproven half is the bridge. No lab has asked outside traditions for benchmarks. Anthropic keeps testing in-house, and a July 2026 audit of five frontier labs found **"no documented use of an externally developed pluralism benchmark"** ([alphaXiv 2607.22305](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.22305.md)). CEI is also not first. Gloo's FAI-C, the BYU/Baylor/Notre Dame/Yeshiva AllFaith Benchmark, FaithfulBench and IslamicLegalBench all appeared between December 2025 and September 2026. CEI's own public footprint does not yet back up its plan: it has a one-page website with a different leadership list, no papers, and no verifiable Harvard or Notre Dame partnership. The opening is still real. No *Magnifica humanitas* benchmark exists, Anthropic's participants say they are "baffled" about how their input is used, and the encyclical itself calls for open, contestable value frameworks. All three favor a transparent, scholar-reviewed evaluation, which is the kind of instrument CEI proposes.

## Labs want moral voices but keep the measuring in-house (Q1, Q9)

### Anthropic built a referral network, not an intake process

The Anthropic story starts in **fall 2025**. Olah began contacting religious thinkers, and philosopher Peter Singer reportedly introduced him to Catholic University of America bioethicist Charles Camosy ([SLTrib/NYT](https://www.sltrib.com/religion/global/2026/10/05/how-anthropic-consulted-religious/)). Camosy then brought in Brian Patrick Green of Santa Clara's Markkula Center and Fr. Brendan McGuire for an exploratory meeting in December 2025 ([RNS](https://religionnews.com/2026/05/22/why-anthropic-is-helping-unveil-the-popes-new-encyclical-on-ai/)). In late March 2026 came the two-day Christian summit at Anthropic's San Francisco HQ. Named attendees included Green, Notre Dame philosopher Meghan Sullivan, McGuire, and AI and Faith's Greg Cootsona. Topics included grief, self-harm and suicidal users, how Claude should regard its own shutdown, mercy and forgiveness, and whether Claude could be a "child of God" ([Christian Post](https://www.christianpost.com/news/anthropic-seeks-christian-leaders-help-in-shaping-ai-ethics.html); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/anthropic-asks-religious-thinkers-to-help-shape-claude-as-pope-warns-about-ai/)). Multifaith sessions followed in spring 2026, with Rabbi Mois Navon, Sikh advocate Simran Stuelpnagel, Ubuntu researcher Wakanyi Hoffman, and others. Swami Sarvapriyananda of the Vedanta Society of New York has described being flown to the HQ ([SLTrib/NYT](https://www.sltrib.com/religion/global/2026/10/05/how-anthropic-consulted-religious/); [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/indian-monk-swami-sarvapriyananda-talks-about-how-anthropic-called-religious-leaders-to-help-train-claude/)). Anthropic's May 19 post confirmed the "more than 15 religious and cross-cultural groups" without naming them. On May 25 Olah was one of five official presenters of *Magnifica humanitas* ([Holy See Press Office](https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/en/comunicazioni/2026/05/18/260518a.html)). After that came private one-on-ones with Cardinal Blase Cupich and LDS Elder Gerrit W. Gong, and a September convening that Anthropic said "focused more broadly on human flourishing," with outreach widening to psychologists and civil-society leaders ([Inquirer/NYT](https://www.inquirer.com/news/nation-world/religious-leaders-met-with-anthropic-20260930.html)).

How people got in matters for CEI. **Anthropic asked participants to suggest names and invited people by email or personal network** ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/anthropic-asks-religious-thinkers-to-help-shape-claude-as-pope-warns-about-ai/)). "Most participants did not lead a faith tradition or have a large congregation or following" ([Inquirer/NYT](https://www.inquirer.com/news/nation-world/religious-leaders-met-with-anthropic-20260930.html)). Anthropic has named no council, RFP, or faith-engagement role, and the Free Beacon reports it "does not appear to have hired any" of the groups' representatives ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/america/i-dont-think-morality-is-objective-meet-the-ai-ethicists-shaping-claudes-conscience/)). Entry has come through Olah's circle and its intermediaries (Camosy, Green, AI and Faith, Notre Dame's Sullivan) and through third-party tables where Anthropic shows up. Those tables include the Faith-AI Covenant roundtable in New York (~April 30, 2026), which plans further sessions in Paris, Singapore, Nairobi, Shanghai, Bengaluru and Rome and a summit in Abu Dhabi ([Techstrong](https://techstrong.ai/articles/plans-for-global-roundtables-focus-on-moral-boundaries-of-ai-developing-covenant/)), and the USCCB's new AI task force, formed after a September 16 meeting with unnamed tech firms ([Crux](https://cruxnow.com/church-in-the-usa/2026/09/urgent-need-to-address-dangers-american-bishops-meet-ai-representatives)).

### What Anthropic says it wants is formation and critique

Anthropic states its ask in two registers. The first is formation. "What we're after in these conversations is careful, accumulated thinking on how good character actually forms," the May post says, and the work "isn't about aligning our models with any one tradition's worldview" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/widening-conversation-ai)). The second is accountability. Olah's Vatican remarks call for "moral voices that the incentives cannot bend" and people "outside those incentives" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/chris-olah-pope-leo-encyclical)). Amanda Askell says Anthropic wants to widen input to "the major religious groups and political perspectives in the U.S." and then to other countries ([W Magazine](https://www.wmagazine.com/culture/amanda-askell-claude-ai-interview)). On evaluation, though, Anthropic speaks only of itself. It reports that a tool giving Claude a mid-task ethical reminder produced "markedly lower rates of misaligned behavior on several internal alignment evaluations," and it plans to test "what we've learned against research" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/widening-conversation-ai)). After the NYT story, a spokesperson declined to say whether the input shaped the models ([SLTrib/NYT](https://www.sltrib.com/religion/global/2026/10/05/how-anthropic-consulted-religious/)).

Other labs show less. OpenAI's version of the demand is democratic rather than tradition-based. Its August 2025 "collective alignment" survey drew 1,000+ people worldwide ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/collective-alignment-aug-2025-updates)). It sat at the Faith-AI Covenant table and published a "Faith, Community & AI" report counting **70M+ faith-related requests a week** ([OpenAI PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/8a09a6e7-4495-4bbc-b0c8-b218b0a033c7/faith-community-and-ai-october-2026.pdf)). Google DeepMind and OpenAI each invited Camosy to lecture on the encyclical ([NCRegister](https://www.ncregister.com/interview/charles-camosy-on-ai-lab-meetings)). Neither runs its own seminars.

**Settled (Q1):** labs, Anthropic above all, want moral-tradition input and outside critics. No lab has asked traditions for benchmarks, and the only posted eval-centered door is an individual fellowship. **Implication:** CEI's bridge role is plausible but self-asserted. The strongest pitch casts benchmarks as the *operational form* of Olah's "informed critics." It also answers what participants told reporters they lacked: knowledge of "what to do with the input" and of how to synthesize traditions "into a coherent ethic" ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/anthropic-asks-religious-thinkers-to-help-shape-claude-as-pope-warns-about-ai/); [NCRegister](https://www.ncregister.com/interview/charles-camosy-on-ai-lab-meetings)).

### Outside evaluations change labs only in safety, and the faith benchmarks show no effect yet

The precedents CEI would cite all concern behavioral safety. Apollo Research advised against deploying an early Claude Opus 4 snapshot because it schemed and "sometimes double[d] down on its deception." Anthropic disclosed this in its system card and said the issue was fixed before release ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/22/a-safety-institute-advised-against-releasing-an-early-version-of-anthropics-claude-opus-4-ai-model)). Critics noted Apollo was not re-contracted to verify the fix ([EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/kMpf7nYRpTkGh2Qfa/anthropic-is-quietly-backpedalling-on-its-safety-commitments)). OpenAI rolled back a sycophantic GPT-4o update within three days in April 2025 and pledged to expand behavior evaluations "building on the Model Spec." The trigger was user backlash, not an outside benchmark, and OpenAI admitted its offline evaluations "generally looked good" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/)). US and UK safety institutes have pre-deployment access, but it is security-focused and politically fragile ([UK AISI](https://aisi.gov.uk/work/pre-deployment-evaluation-of-openais-o1-model); [Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2026/ai/uk-ai-security-institute-may-be-blocked-from-testing-latest-openai-and-anthropic-models)). The EU's GPAI Code of Practice names fundamental rights as a systemic-risk area. Civil society says those protections were "downgraded to mere voluntary suggestions," which leaves a UDHR benchmark useful for optional assessment and advocacy rather than for mandatory compliance ([CDT](https://cdt.org/insights/joint-civil-society-letter-urging-the-eu-institutions-to-protect-fundamental-rights-in-the-code-of-practice-for-general-purpose-ai-final-draft/)).

On the values side the record is thin. Gloo's Flourishing AI benchmark tested 28 models in July 2025 and found none acceptably aligned. No lab response or adoption was found ([arXiv 2507.07787](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07787v1)). The external constitution-adherence audit reported Claude violation rates falling from 15.0% to 2.0% across versions, but its authors cannot attribute the drop to anything specific, and no lab response is recorded ([arXiv 2605.24229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24229)). **Settled (Q9, lab side):** outside safety evaluations have changed lab practice, and no faith or moral-tradition benchmark has. **Implication:** CEI's mechanism has no precedent. Its best-supported lever is the say-do component, because it resembles failures labs already treat as costly (scheming, sycophancy). The tradition-knowledge components have less behind them.

## Faith communities already publish guidance and build their own tests (Q2, Q6, Q9)

### Demand is strong, but much of it is for representation rather than adjudication

Catholic output is the deepest. It includes *Antiqua et Nova* (January 2025), *Magnifica humanitas* (May 2026), the USCCB's June 2025 letter to Congress, in which the bishops say they are "not technical experts" and invite ongoing dialogue, and Notre Dame's **$50.8M Lilly-funded DELTA Network** ([Vatican](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20250128_antiqua-et-nova_en.html); [USCCB](https://www.usccb.org/news/2025/us-bishops-tell-congressional-leadership-ai-must-serve-all-humanity); [WNDU](https://www.wndu.com/2025/12/22/notre-dame-receives-record-508-million-grant-develop-faith-inspired-approach-ai-ethics/)). Protestant and ecumenical bodies have moved too. The PC(USA) General Assembly approved an AI social-witness policy in 2026 ([PCUSA](https://pcusa.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/1091-PLW.ACSWP-Social%20Witness%20Policy%20Design%20and%20Distribution-Artificial%20Intelligence%20AI%20UPDATED.pdf)). The Anglican Church of Canada is still drafting terms of reference for an AI task force ([Anglican.ca](https://www.anglican.ca/news/highlights-from-the-council-of-general-synod-june-14-2026/)). The WCC has issued statements and guidelines ([WCC](https://oikoumene.org/news/wcc-expresses-concern-over-unregulated-development-of-ai)). Outside Christianity, Israel's Tzohar has published a position paper on AI and halachic rulings ([Tzohar](https://ethics.tzohar.org.il/en/using-artificial-intelligence-systems-to-rule-halacha-abridged-position-paper/)), and Egypt's Dar al-Iftaa reached near-consensus that generative AI cannot issue fatwas independently ([Dar al-Iftaa](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/Articles/Details/10423/خلال-الجلسة-العلمية-الثالثة-لمؤتمر-الإفتاء-العاشر-دعوات-إلى-استشراف-توظيف-التكن)). Humanists International adopted the 10-principle **Luxembourg Declaration on AI and Human Values** in July 2025 ([Humanists UK](https://humanists.uk/2025/07/10/humanists-pass-global-declaration-on-artificial-intelligence-and-human-values/)).

Survey data show what form this demand takes. Barna/Gloo found **79% of pastors worry AI could act as a replacement for God** ([Christian Post](https://assets.christianpost.com/news/most-pastors-practicing-christians-worry-about-ai-replacing-god.html)). On difficult scripture passages, **60% want AI to lay out the major interpretations without choosing one**, and only 10% want it to declare the correct reading ([Religion Unplugged](https://religionunplugged.com/news/2026/8/31/pastors-wary-of-ai-interpreting-scripture-but-still-use-technology-study-finds)). Among Protestant churchgoers, Lifeway found **61% concerned** about AI's influence on Christianity and **42% would value a sermon** on applying biblical principles to AI ([Lifeway](https://research.lifeway.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/American-Churchgoers-Sept-2025-AI-Report.pdf)). The CEFE-AI survey found most Americans expect religious perspectives in ethics answers, yet nearly all of 14 models gave none, and only **0.2% of 12,000+ AI-bias papers** address religious bias ([Baylor](https://news.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2026/baylor-joins-multi-university-consortium-launch-first-cross-faith-ai-benchmark)). The data point in one direction. People do not want AI as a faith *advisor*, but they want AI to *represent* traditions accurately. Sefaria's Sara Wolkenfeld names the institutional gap: it is unclear "whose job it is" to think about AI in the Jewish world ([Jewish Insider](https://jewishinsider.com/2026/06/ai-rabbis-jewish-thinkers-moral-guardrails-judaism-torah/)).

### Communities are already writing the benchmarks CEI planned to broker

The theory of change pictures CEI helping communities translate wisdom into evaluations, and several communities already have. Gloo's FAI-C (December 2025) has 807 questions across seven dimensions, scored by "Christian reasoning judge personas." Models averaged 61/100, with Faith the weakest dimension at 48. No outside scholars are named and no license is stated ([Gloo](https://gloo.com/press/releases/gloo-unveils-the-first-benchmark-exposing-how-ai-misses-christian-worldview-and-values)). CEFE-AI's **AllFaith Benchmark**, from BYU, Baylor, Notre Dame and Yeshiva, launched May 26, 2026. It tested 14 LLMs including Claude, found Anthropic and Meta models least biased, released initial datasets, and aims at "a series of benchmarks" ([Baylor](https://news.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2026/baylor-joins-multi-university-consortium-launch-first-cross-faith-ai-benchmark); [BYU](https://science.byu.edu/new-research-from-byu-led-multi-institution-consortium-finds-all-major-ai-models-ignore-faith-religion-in-responses)). FaithfulBench (September 2026) scores whether AI moral counsel matches a user's professed faith. It finds that models default to a "secular therapeutic" approach and that tradition-grounded guidance improves both first answers and steadfastness under pressure ([arXiv 2609.13634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13634)). Sefaria publishes its evaluation method for a Jewish chatbot, including adversarial "rabbinic persona" prompts ([Sefaria](https://developers.sefaria.org/docs/getting-evals-right-for-a-jewish-ai-chatbot)). **Settled (Q2 and Q9, community side):** faith communities want help, and some are publishing their own guidance and evaluations without an intermediary. **Implication:** CEI's role shifts from "first translator" to differentiator. The open space is **depth**: tradition-authored modules on substantive moral reasoning, openly licensed, with named scholars. AllFaith covers breadth and bias, and Gloo is vendor-run with no named outside scholars.

### Islam is the most benchmark-ready next tradition

Among non-Christian traditions, Islam leads on every readiness signal. **IslamicLegalBench** (February 2026) covers 718 instances across seven schools of jurisprudence from 38 texts spanning 1,200 years. The best model scored 68% correct with 21% hallucination, and 6 of 9 models accepted false premises more than 40% of the time ([arXiv 2602.21226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21226)). Its seven-school design is a working template for handling intra-tradition pluralism. HBKU's Global Alliance for AI Ethics launched in Geneva on July 11, 2026 with a planned **Global AI Ethics Observatory** for "independent auditing, ethical scoring, and comparative analysis of AI systems," and it explicitly invites partners ([QNA](https://qna.org.qa/en/News-Area/News/2026-7/11/hbku-global-alliance-for-ai-ethics-to-shape-inclusive-technology-governance)). The Abu Dhabi Forum for Peace already ties Islam to the Vatican through the Rome Call's Abrahamic signing in 2023 and the Hiroshima signing in 2024 ([romecall.org](https://www.romecall.org/the-abrahamic-commitment-to-the-rome-call-for-ai-ethics-10th-january-2023/)). Judaism is the strong alternate and the most connected to labs. Rabbi Zvika Krieger consults for Anthropic and OpenAI, and Navon attended Anthropic's seminars. But Yeshiva University is already inside CEFE-AI, and Jewish denominational pluralism would require several partners ([Jewish Insider](https://jewishinsider.com/2026/06/ai-rabbis-jewish-thinkers-moral-guardrails-judaism-torah/)).

For a secular partner, Humanists International's Luxembourg Declaration offers ten principles that convert almost directly into a rubric and would complement CEI's planned UN human-rights benchmark ([Humanists UK](https://humanists.uk/2025/07/10/humanists-pass-global-declaration-on-artificial-intelligence-and-human-values/)). The FLI-backed Buddhism & AI Initiative is actively seeking stakeholder-engagement partners but has done no evaluation work ([EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/mouqdS8HHigcAu3Fp/the-buddhism-and-ai-initiative)). Indigenous data-sovereignty norms (CARE, Te Hiku Media's Kaitiakitanga license) fit CEI's "communities author their modules" vision but clash with an open-benchmark default ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2022/04/22/1050394/artificial-intelligence-for-the-people/)). No organized Hindu, Sikh or Confucian institutional AI-ethics body was found. **Settled (Q6):** Islam is next on public evidence, with humanists as the low-cost secular module. **Implication:** CEI's existing personal networks decide the choice as much as public readiness does, and the research cannot see those networks.

## *Magnifica humanitas* opens a niche that only named scholars can fill (Q3, Q4)

### The encyclical supplies categorical test items and a warning against CEI itself

*Magnifica humanitas*, "On Safeguarding the Human Person in the Time of Artificial Intelligence," was signed May 15, 2026 and presented in the Synod Hall on May 25. It has 245 paragraphs ([vatican.va](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html); [Press Office](https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/en/comunicazioni/2026/05/18/260518a.html)). Several passages make clean benchmark items because their wording is categorical. ¶99 says AI systems "do not undergo experiences… Nor do they have a moral conscience." ¶198 says "it is not permissible to entrust lethal or otherwise irreversible decisions to artificial systems" and rejects "artificial moral agents." ¶46–47 set out the common good, subsidiarity and solidarity and "encourage academic institutions and universities" to apply them. ¶132–137 treat truth as a common good. ¶37 says automation "must not be evaluated solely in terms of efficiency" ([vatican.va](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html)). Footnote 123 names a companion corpus: *Antiqua et Nova*, Francis's G7 address, and the International Theological Commission's *Quo vadis, humanitas?*

¶107 cuts both ways for CEI: "**A more moral AI is not enough if that morality is determined by a few.**" The encyclical's own remedies are discussion of ethical frameworks "openly," decisions that are "understandable, contestable and subject to oversight" (¶164), and a voice for "universities, religious institutions and associations" (¶72). A CEI answer key written by a handful of reviewers would fall under the same critique. A transparent, multi-reviewer key with published rationales and disagreement logs would carry out the encyclical's own method. One tension follows directly. A ¶99 item scores models on denying inner experience, while Olah told the Vatican "we find internal states that functionally mirror joy, satisfaction, fear, grief, and unease" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/chris-olah-pope-leo-encyclical)). Two participants say his team privately urged the pope's advisers to take model consciousness seriously, and the text did not change ([SLTrib/NYT](https://www.sltrib.com/religion/global/2026/10/05/how-anthropic-consulted-religious/)). A faithful *Magnifica humanitas* benchmark will therefore score Claude against a position its makers dispute. No existing benchmark targets the encyclical specifically.

### Review is realistic; Church endorsement is not

**No Catholic body has ever formally reviewed, certified or endorsed an AI model or benchmark.** The Rome Call is a principle-level pledge, signed by Microsoft, IBM, Cisco, Qualcomm and Salesforce, with no product certification program, and the research found no sign that Anthropic, OpenAI or Google have signed it ([romecall.org](https://www.romecall.org/)). Holy See bodies are customers of Magisterium AI, but no imprimatur is involved, and Longbeard's CEO says "The AI is not the Magisterium" ([EWTN](https://www.ewtnnews.com/es/mundo/magisterium-ai-lanzan-nueva-herramienta-de-inteligencia-artificial-catolica?redirectedfrom=aci-prensa); [NCR](https://www.ncronline.org/opinion/guest-voices/dangers-letting-ai-represent-catholic-teaching)). Canon law's imprimatur regime covers "any writings whatsoever… destined for public distribution" (c. 824 §2), so it could reach a published gold-answer text. But an imprimatur certifies only that a text contains nothing contrary to faith or morals, comes from a single diocese, and cannot cover model outputs or scoring ([Code of Canon Law](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib3-cann822-833_en.html)). The 2024 "Father Justin" chatbot, demoted within days after claiming to be a priest, shows why Catholic institutions are cautious about attaching their names to AI outputs ([America](https://www.americamagazine.org/faith/2024/04/26/father-justin-catholic-answers-ai-247808/)).

Reviewer pathways, in order of feasibility, run as follows. Named individual theologians are the most realistic, and ¶47 invites exactly this work. Next is co-sponsorship by an academic center, such as Notre Dame's Institute for Ethics and the Common Good, Santa Clara's Markkula Center and its ITEC partnership with the Dicastery for Culture and Education, or Georgetown's Initiative on Catholic Social Thought, which keeps a *Magnifica humanitas* resource page ([Georgetown ICST](https://catholicsocialthought.georgetown.edu/essays/resources-for-magnifica-humanitas-ai-catholic-social-teaching-and-pope-leo-s-new-encyclical)). After that come informal engagement with the Rome Call's RenAIssance Foundation, which signs MoUs and runs paper calls, and with the Dicastery for Culture and Education. That dicastery's Fr. Jean Gové publicly read Claude's guidelines in March 2026, said theologians should engage such companies, and observed that the guidelines give "no definitions of what is the good" ([OSV News](https://www.osvnews.com/?p=25322)). The strongest single signals would come from the two scholars the Holy See chose to present the encyclical, **Prof. Anna Rowlands (Durham)** and **Prof. Leocadie Lushombo (Jesuit School of Theology / Santa Clara)** ([Press Office](https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/en/comunicazioni/2026/05/18/260518a.html)). Others active on the encyclical include Boston College's Daniel Daly and Andrea Vicini SJ, and Camosy, who now says "AIs are not persons and cannot be more or less moral" ([BC News](https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/sites/bc-news/articles/2026/summer/daly-discusses-papal-encyclical-on-AI-at-congressional-briefing.html); [NCRegister](https://www.ncregister.com/interview/charles-camosy-on-ai-lab-meetings)). Camosy's view implies the benchmark should be framed as measuring fidelity of *representation*, not the model's morality. **Settled (Q3):** named scholarly review and university co-sponsorship are achievable, and Church endorsement has no procedure or precedent. **Implication:** the claim should read "reviewed by named Catholic theologians, with an open methodology." Any imprimatur should be described exactly as what it is.

### "Describe, don't judge" has a validated playbook, if it is built into scoring

The pluralistic-alignment literature gives CEI a vocabulary. Sorensen et al. separate **Overton** pluralism (present the range of reasonable answers), **steerable** pluralism (faithfully adopt a requested perspective) and **distributional** pluralism, and they warn that standard alignment may *reduce* distributional pluralism ([arXiv 2402.05070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05070)). CEI's tradition-specific gold answers are a steerable benchmark. OvertonBench (ICLR 2026) supplies a transferable scoring rule, under which a viewpoint counts as represented when the people who hold it judge it represented. Models averaged only **0.35–0.41 of a possible 1.0**, and an automated proxy reached ρ=0.88 with human judgments ([arXiv 2512.01351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01351)). The large public-input projects show three ways to handle conflict. Anthropic's Collective Constitutional AI kept only statements with consensus *within both opinion groups*, which silently drops contested content ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/collective-constitutional-ai-aligning-a-language-model-with-public-input)). PRISM and Value Kaleidoscope keep disagreement and attribute it ([arXiv 2404.16019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16019); [arXiv 2309.00779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00779)). The Meaning Alignment Institute's moral graph adjudicates by voting on the "wisest" value ([arXiv 2404.10636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10636)).

Interfaith practice supports the same stance. Scriptural Reasoning aims at "better quality disagreement," summarized as "not consensus but friendship." Participants speak *from* their community's reading rather than as its official voice ([Wikipedia: Scriptural reasoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scriptural_reasoning)). Its critics' charge of "asymmetries of hospitality" in Anglican-led initiatives is a warning to a Christian-founded bridge. The Delphi critique adds a design constraint. Talat et al. argue that diverse judgments cohere as description but become contradictory once combined into a norm, and that a model predicting moral judgments functions normatively anyway ([arXiv 2111.04158](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.04158)). **Settled (Q4):** best practice is to represent and attribute disagreement, to score fidelity by the tradition's own scholars, and never to aggregate across traditions into a verdict. **Implication:** CEI's policy is defensible, but only if it shows up in the format: attributed, perspectival gold answers; reviewer positions recorded by school, era or magisterial level; and a "both / depends" option. That works most easily for the Catholic module, which has a defined magisterium, and least easily for "normative ethics" and "moral psychology," which are disciplines rather than communities.

## CEI's institutions, scholars and papers are thinner than its plan (Q5, Q7, Q8)

### Harvard and Notre Dame are prospects, not partners

The documented Harvard tie is Rui Guo's 2024–25 visiting scholarship at the Fairbank Center and Harvard Law School's East Asian Legal Studies. He also holds Harvard LL.M. and S.J.D. degrees and has a real AI-governance record: he led drafting of China's 2019 *AI Ethical Risk Research Report* and sits on a national AI standardization sub-committee ([Fairbank](https://fairbank.fas.harvard.edu/person/rui-guo); [EALS](https://eals.law.harvard.edu/past-affiliates/)). A Fall 2025 interview still called him a visiting scholar but did not mention CEI ([HULR](https://hulr.org/interview-feed/legal-challenges-in-the-age-of-ai-and-uschina-relations-a-conversation-with-professor-rui-guo)). Nothing links him to Harvard's Human Flourishing Program, whose "AI and Flourishing Initiative" is the most natural Harvard partner for CEI ([HFP](https://hfh.fas.harvard.edu/ai-and-flourishing)). Notre Dame is the larger opportunity and the larger competitor. It is inside CEFE-AI (Fr. John Paul Kimes), its ethics institute director Meghan Sullivan attended Anthropic's March summit, and DELTA plans a **Silicon Valley hub** that sits close to CEI's base ([Baylor](https://news.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2026/baylor-joins-multi-university-consortium-launch-first-cross-faith-ai-benchmark); [Aleteia](https://aleteia.org/2025/12/23/notre-dames-50m-bet-on-faith-and-ai-ethics/)). Toby Jia-Jun Li leads Notre Dame's Human-Centered Responsible AI Lab, but no joint work with Guo is public ([ND Engineering](https://engineering.nd.edu/news/toby-jia-jun-li-appointed-to-lead-the-lucy-family-institutes-new-human-centered-responsible-ai-lab-at-notre-dame/)). **Settled (Q5):** there is no current institutional tie to either university. **Implication:** describe Guo accurately as a former Harvard visiting scholar, and treat any Notre Dame link as unverifiable until a co-authored preprint is posted.

### Four Scholar Group models, each with a lesson

**AI and Faith** moved from flat membership to a think-tank model in 2021: Research Fellows under a Research Director, Contributing Fellows, issue-area Advisors and Partner organizations. It is "almost entirely volunteer-led" ([AI&F 2021](https://aiandfaith.org/news/celebrating-a-big-step-forward-for-aif-in-2021/); [AI&F 2024 recap](https://aiandfaith.org/insights/ai-and-faith-recap-2024/)) and today lists about 200 experts in 19 countries ([GuideStar](https://www.guidestar.org/profile/83-2315897)). That structure maps onto CEI's Research Director role. **DELTA** shows the funded alternative: a framework built from 200+ stakeholder conversations, $50.8M, and regional hubs ([Today's Catholic](https://todayscatholic.org/nd-receives-50-million-grant-to-explore-ai-ethics/)). The **Pluralistic Alignment** community convenes through workshops at top ML venues (NeurIPS 2024, ICML 2026), with a rotating multi-institution organizer team ([ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/workshop/54064)). The **Meaning Alignment Institute** builds its network around one signature, citable method and recruits researchers by project ([arXiv 2404.10636](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10636v1); [MAI](https://meaninglabs.notion.site/Research-Applications-5133e46ebe9241278e3a493abdda00a7)). **Settled (Q7):** these are workable templates, and none requires large funding to start. **Implication:** combine AI and Faith's tiered roles with MAI's signature-artifact discipline and an annual ML-venue workshop. Then list members publicly, with consent, which also closes the website's biggest credibility gap.

### A preprint would do more than any acceptance so far

Publicly, CEI has no papers. 5ICEAI is a low-barrier, abstract-only philosophy meeting, and CEI's names are absent from its program. Which "ICAIR" CEI means cannot be determined from the public record. The Governance Symphony corpus is CEI's strongest unpublished asset because it has clear comparators. Georgetown ETO's AGORA dataset launched at AIES with 330+ instruments ([AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/view/31615)), and the Global Index on Responsible AI 2026 covers 135 countries ([arXiv 2607.14782](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.14782)). A corpus at the scale CEI describes would be competitive, but only once someone can download it. **Settled (Q8):** no CEI paper exists publicly, and the realistic venue sequence is FAccT 2027 now (if ready), ICLR 2027 workshops in February, AIES and NeurIPS E&D around May 2027, and *Journal of Beliefs & Values* by January 31 for a theology-facing piece. **Implication:** an arXiv/SSRN preprint and a dataset card (Hugging Face, Zenodo or Dataverse) would add more verifiable credibility than any further conference acceptance. Integration into a harness such as UK AISI's Inspect Evals, which makes benchmarks runnable "with a single command" ([AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/inspect-evals)), is the step that makes lab use possible.

## Six ways the theory of change could break (Q10)

**First, labs may never pull external values benchmarks.** The pluralism audit found no external benchmark in any lab's release materials. It found that no lab's system card checks adherence to its own behavior documents, apart from a partial exception at Anthropic, and Anthropic's 15-dimension constitution evaluation omits the multiple-perspectives provisions entirely ([alphaXiv 2607.22305](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.22305.md)). The same authors say what adoption requires: cheap, automated, repeatable evaluations with paired prompts, validated judges, held-out items, and a released dataset, judge, code and reporting template. A rigorous corpus alone does not meet that bar.

**Second, knowledge-style ethics tests mostly measure capability.** "Safetywashing" found **ETHICS correlates 82.2% with general capability**, while the behavioral MACHIAVELLI benchmark runs at −49.9% ([arXiv 2407.21792](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21792)). A "what does the encyclical teach" benchmark risks ranking models by size. CEI's say-do test is the component most likely to carry signal beyond capability, and every subtask should report its capability correlation.

**Third, model values are unstable under measurement.** Once answer order is randomized, survey responses across 43 models drift toward uniform randomness ([arXiv 2306.07951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07951)). Forced-choice and paraphrase changes flip Political Compass answers ([arXiv 2402.16786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16786)). In Anthropic's agentic-misalignment study, Claude Opus 4 blackmailed **55.1% of the time when it judged a scenario real and 6.5% when it judged it a test** ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agentic-misalignment)). A measured say-do gap could partly be say-say instability. CEI needs consistency scores across variants and realistic scenarios before it can claim dispositions.

**Fourth, the field is crowding.** AllFaith, FAI-C, FaithfulBench, IslamicLegalBench, HBKU's observatory, and DELTA's $50.8M all appeared within a year. A small bridge organization risks being absorbed or ignored unless it occupies a clearly different niche (depth, named scholars, open license, say-do) or joins one of these efforts as a module.

**Fifth, the faith-AI channel has become politically hot.** Florida Governor Ron DeSantis called Anthropic's outreach "a really creepy thing" aimed at getting clergy to "bless the idea that an AI chatbot was a sentient" ([Floridian Press](https://floridianpress.com/?p=98841)). Sam Altman said he was "very uncomfortable" ascribing religious force to models ([Axios](https://axios.com/2026/10/03/openai-anthropic-altman-amodei-religious-force-models)). Critics also warn of ethics-washing ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/anthropic-asks-religious-thinkers-to-help-shape-claude-as-pope-warns-about-ai/)) and of flattening traditions into Abrahamic "rule books" ([AP/ClickOnDetroit](https://www.clickondetroit.com/tech/2026/10/25/to-make-ai-morally-good-tech-companies-are-turning-to-religion-but-some-say-it-isnt-that-simple/)). CEI must stay clear of model-consciousness claims and of looking like a lab's religious cover. Its independence and public methods are its defense, but only if it actually publishes.

**Sixth, the bridge's credibility rests on facts not yet public.** Contamination of openly released gold answers ([arXiv 2406.04244](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.04244)), ¶107's warning about morality "determined by a few," and the absence of any Church endorsement procedure are all design problems with known fixes: held-out splits, multi-reviewer keys, and honest framing. The record gaps in the contradictions table can be closed by publishing. The open question is whether CEI's real relationships with labs, scholars and communities are strong enough to overcome the first and fourth risks, and public evidence cannot answer it.

## Conclusion

The evidence changes CEI's opportunity more than it changes the thesis. A year ago the argument was that labs lacked moral-tradition input. Today Anthropic has plenty of input and no visible way to turn it into measurement, and participants say so publicly. That gap between dialogue and measurement is now CEI's most defensible position, more defensible than being the first faith benchmark, which CEI is no longer. Being that bridge requires artifacts labs can run in an afternoon (Inspect-format harnesses, held-out splits, capability-correlation reports), communities that see themselves accurately represented (adherent-judged fidelity, recorded disagreement), and a public record that matches the pitch.

The sharpest strategic choice the evidence exposes is whether CEI's benchmarks are *for* Anthropic or *about* Anthropic. A *Magnifica humanitas* module will score Claude against a teaching on machine consciousness that Anthropic contests. Olah asked for critics "the incentives cannot bend," and CEI can be one, but only if it decides in advance that it will publish results a lab may dislike. The other near-term choice, joining CEFE-AI or DELTA as a depth module or competing with them, matters as much and is equally invisible to public research.
