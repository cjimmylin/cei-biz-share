# Consistency and fact-check review: the three CEI plan versions

Reviewed Oct 27, 2026. Scope: `plan/BUSINESS-PLAN-2027-2029.md` (general, 570 lines), `plan/TECHNICAL-BRIEF.md` (223 lines), `plan/CHRISTIAN-EDITION.md` (88 lines), checked against `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` (Jimmy's Oct 27 answers), `reports/CEI theory of change evidence.md`, the research notes under `research_notes/`, the Governance Symphony poster text and leadership notes under `source/meta-muse/`, the Praxis workbook findings, the scholar-network README, `OPPORTUNITIES.md` and `README.md`. Line numbers refer to the snapshot files as of this date. "[verify]" marks a claim I could not settle from the files on hand; "[JIMMY]" marks a decision only the founders can make.

## Headline findings

1. **The Governance Symphony numbers are overstated in all three versions.** Every version says 5,140 statements were "mapped" into six clusters. The poster's own figures show 5,140 statements were *collected*; the six cluster sizes sum to 2,646, and 374 principles x 2,694 statements = the 1,007,556 cells the poster reports as scored. So about 2,700 statements have been coded and 2,646 clustered. The general plan's own internal checklist (line 544) already says this; the body text does not.
2. **The Scholar Group is described as existing in some places and as not yet contacted in others.** The general plan says it "already meets" (line 103) and "convenes scholars" (line 15), the technical brief says it "is recruiting from about 650 identified scholars" (line 187), yet the general plan also says "outreach begins Q1 2027" (lines 146, 220), the scholar-network README says "No one has been contacted," and `plan/02-programs.md` says "no outreach yet."
3. **Lab demand is attributed to "the labs" when the evidence supports one lab's co-founder.** The evidence report is explicit: "No lab has asked outside traditions for benchmarks." The general plan opens with "The labs themselves are asking" (line 11) and lists "lab demand for outside critics" as a why-now driver (line 78); the technical brief says "Labs have asked for this kind of outside check" (line 63).
4. **"Co-authors," "partners" and "talks" are used loosely.** The co-author claim is supported by the poster (Toby Li at Notre Dame; Jenny Zhu and Rui Guo listed at Harvard), but the Christian edition adds "and review our work" and says "we work alongside" AllFaith; the general plan says "partnership talks with AllFaith" and "working relationships" with Green, Camosy and AI and Faith, where Jimmy's answer is "knows."
5. **The 12-month clock starts in different places.** The technical brief and Christian edition count from (first-tranche) funding; the general plan's executive summary, program table and impact table count from an unstated date, and line 254 assumes results by Q4 2027.
6. **Board arithmetic does not add up inside the general plan.** "4-5 founding board members" (line 34), "founding cohort of 3-4" plus a chair (lines 335-336), "Board of about 6" (line 297), six named seat categories (line 341) and three filed directors (line 331) cannot all be true.
7. **The epiplexity "paper" is an abstract.** 5ICEAI is abstract-only and non-archival; the program lists only the Harvard-affiliated talk by Hanzhen (Jenny) Zhu, the poster's co-author. Which ICAIR is meant is unknown. Both versions that mention it say "paper ... accepted at two conferences."
8. **Facts the Praxis application states differently are still unreconciled**: founded 2025 vs "founded 2024" in the Muse draft and the website; 30 part-time vs "about 40 volunteers"; $7.5K vs "$7K+"; "Co-Founder & CEO" vs "President." The plan's own checklist lists these but the body picks one side without saying so.
9. **The general plan ships with an internal checklist** (lines 537-555, "remove before sending") inside a document whose live version is the one shared with board candidates.
10. **All three documents are dated Oct 26** but contain facts confirmed on Oct 27 (the checklist says so at line 550), and the README says snapshots are as of Oct 27.

## 2. Claims not supported by the evidence on file

Each item: the quoted phrase, why it is unsupported, and safer wording.

1. **"The labs themselves are asking for 'moral voices that the incentives cannot bend'"** (G11). The evidence report: "labs, Anthropic above all, want moral-tradition input and outside critics. No lab has asked traditions for benchmarks." Only one lab's co-founder has said this publicly; OpenAI's version is a public survey, and DeepMind, Meta, xAI and Mistral show nothing comparable. Safer: "Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah has asked publicly for 'moral voices that the incentives cannot bend.'"

2. **"lab demand for outside critics"** as a why-now driver (G78). Same problem, and it sits beside two verifiable drivers (regulation, funding), which lends it their weight. Safer: "a frontier lab's public call for outside critics."

3. **"Labs have asked for this kind of outside check."** (T63). The brief corrects itself two lines later ("the ask has been for dialogue, not tests"), but the first sentence will be the one quoted back. Safer: "A frontier-lab co-founder has asked publicly for outside critics."

4. **"why we publish at top AI venues"** (G86). No CEI paper is published or preprinted (evidence report: "No CEI-authored paper or 'Governance Symphony' preprint exists online"); the only acceptances are an abstract-only philosophy meeting and an unidentified ICAIR. Safer: "why we aim to publish at top AI venues."

5. **"with Notre Dame and Harvard co-authors"** (G23, G209, G218, G319, T191) and **"Notre Dame and Harvard scholars co-author and review our work"** (C52). The poster supports co-authorship: Toby Jia-Jun Li (Notre Dame), Hanzhen Jenny Zhu (Harvard) and Rui Guo (then at Harvard). It does not support "review," and the Harvard half rests on CEI's own co-founder (now a former affiliate, and leaving) plus one person whose Harvard role is not recorded [verify]. The public record shows no CEI link to either university (evidence report, "Harvard and Notre Dame are prospects, not partners"). Safer: "co-authored with researchers at the University of Notre Dame and Harvard" and, in C52, drop "and review our work." Do not list "Governance Symphony co-authors" under a "Harvard" partner row (G109); it reads as an institutional tie.
