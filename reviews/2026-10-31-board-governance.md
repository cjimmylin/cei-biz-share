# Board-chair and governance review: CEI board brief, charters and plan

Reviewed Oct 31, 2026 against `plan/BOARD-BRIEF.md`, the two charters, `plan/BUSINESS-PLAN-2027-2029.md`, the technical brief, the Christian edition, `plan/CEI-Budget-v3-2027-2031.xlsx`, the Oct 27 reviews, `plan/INTERNAL-CHECKLIST.md` and `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`. "Carried over" marks an Oct 27 finding still open; "to verify" marks a rule or fact to confirm with counsel or the source named. Sections cited are the California Corporations Code unless stated.

## 1. Verdict

Not yet. The brief invites me onto "the board" without saying what that board governs. Social Good Fund's handbook says a Model A project has "no legal separation" from the sponsor, that "ultimate legal and financial responsibility sits with Social Good Fund and our Board of Directors," and that the sponsor keeps "final oversight and approval" of employment ([Model A](https://socialgoodfund.gitbook.io/project-handbook/model-a-fiscal-sponsorship.md); [Project Committee](https://socialgoodfund.gitbook.io/project-handbook/relationship-+-roles-under-a-project-committee-model.md)). The plan's own "Legal structure (to confirm)" paragraph describes that model. So until 2029 a candidate joins the board of a corporation that holds no funds, employs no one and may own no data, plus, if the agreement says so, the sponsor's project committee. That is a reasonable thing to join, and much of the set (dated funding tiers, executive session, founder pay at $0, "Still open") is better than most founding boards get. But the brief must say it, and the charters must stop promising powers the board cannot legally give away. With items 1 to 8 fixed, I would take the one-year first term.

## 2. Must fix before this goes to a board candidate

**1. Say which body the candidate joins and what it can decide.** *Carried over* (Oct 27 item 1; the plan promises a paragraph "before any outside director joins," but the brief goes out without it).
*Where:* Brief, "Governance": "fiscally sponsored by Social Good Fund ... The sponsorship terms, including who owns datasets and code, are being confirmed in writing."
*Why:* A director's duties run to the corporation they sit on. If money, staff, grants and IP sit at the sponsor, the board approves budgets it does not control and hires staff it does not employ. Experienced candidates ask on the first call.
*Fix (replace the "Entity" bullet):* "**Entity.** CEI is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation (Articles approved 2026). Its work runs as a fiscally sponsored project of Social Good Fund under a [Model A / Model C] agreement dated [date]. The sponsor holds all funds and grants, employs any staff, holds title to data and code until released under open licenses, charges [X]% of receipts, and has final approval of legal, financial and employment matters. CEI's directors govern the corporation, serve as the project's [committee / advisory body], and set program, policy, budget requests and the leader's hiring subject to the sponsor's approval. We expect our own 501(c)(3) from [2029], when funds, staff and assets transfer. Funds to date ($17K) are held at [where]." Mark each bracket "to confirm" until the executed agreement is in hand.

**2. The brief does not state the commitment, or who is on the board now.**
*Where:* Brief, "What we ask of a board member": "About four meetings a year ... A personal gift that is meaningful to you ... A one-year first term." "Who we are" names only the founders.
*Why:* A recruitment packet gives time, money, liability and term in numbers. This gives none: no hours, no give-or-get range though the plan targets "at least $50K from board members and major donors," nothing on liability or insurance, no term limit or removal rule. It omits that the board today is three insiders, that bylaws and D&O do not yet exist, and whether the candidate must be a practicing Christian.
*Fix (replace the section):* "**What you are committing to.** *Time:* four board meetings a year (two in person), one committee, and five to eight hours a month in the first year. *Money:* a personal gift each year meaningful to you, no minimum; as a board we aim to give or open doors to about $50K in 2027. *Term:* a one-year first term, then staggered three-year terms, at most two consecutive; removal only by two-thirds of the directors in office. *Liability:* before you join, the corporation will have adopted bylaws indemnifying directors to the full extent California allows and bound D&O insurance of at least $1M [to confirm]; unpaid directors are also protected by Corp. Code §5239 when such insurance is in place and by the federal Volunteer Protection Act (both to verify). *Faith:* we ask that the chair and a majority of directors be self-described practicing Christians; no individual seat carries that requirement. **Today:** three directors (Jimmy Lin, Rui Guo, Imran Babar), no independents, no chair, bylaws and insurance to be adopted before you join, no committees." Make room by cutting "What CEI builds" and "Proof so far" by a third; the PDF is two pages, but only a fifth concerns the board.

**3. The Methods Committee charter delegates a power the board cannot delegate, then implies the board cannot take it back.**
*Where:* Charter §1: "The board delegates to the committee final authority over ... decisions are final unless the board finds that carrying them out would break the law or exceed the approved budget." §8: "amended by the board after consulting the committee."
*Why:* Section 5212(b): "A committee exercising the authority of the board shall not include as members persons who are not directors" ([text](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_corp_code_section_5212)); charter §3 bars directors. So the committee is advisory in law, the "delegation" is self-restraint, and §8 lets a simple majority rewrite it in one meeting. The intent is right; the mechanism is wrong.
*Fix (replace the last two sentences of §1 and all of §7):* "The board keeps the legal responsibility California law places on it. It binds itself by this policy: CEI staff and leadership carry out the committee's decisions on (a) to (d) as final. The board sets aside a decision only if two-thirds of the directors in office find, after written notice and 30 days for the committee to reconsider, that carrying it out would break the law, breach a binding agreement, exceed the approved budget, or be withheld by the fiscal sponsor. The board publishes its finding and does not substitute its own method decision. This policy and §8 are amended only by two-thirds of the directors in office after consulting the committee, published 30 days before effect. The Executive Director's annual evaluation includes compliance with this policy."

**4. The president's roles collide, and California's default makes it worse.**
*Where:* Plan, "Team and governance": "the president serves as unpaid interim leader, reporting monthly to the board (proposed; hours to confirm)"; "the ED reports to the board through the chair"; Operating rhythm: "Weekly: Leadership meeting: founders, the ED once hired"; Charter §4: "on the president's nomination."
*Why:* Section 5213(a) makes "the president, or if there is no president the chair of the board ... the general manager and chief executive officer" unless the bylaws say otherwise ([text](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_corp_code_section_5213)). As written the President is CEO by law, interim leader in fact, a voting director, sole nominator of the committee meant to check the founders, and the ED's weekly supervisor, with no end date and no recusal beyond pay.
*Fix:* Bylaws: "The Executive Director is the chief executive officer. The President is an officer of the board and does not act as general manager." Plan and brief: "The president serves as unpaid interim leader at [N] hours a week until the earlier of the ED's start or [Sept 30, 2027], when the board renews or ends the arrangement in executive session. While interim leader, the president does not vote on the ED search, selection or pay, on his own evaluation, or on Methods Committee nominations, and does not supervise the ED once hired." Charter §4: "nominated by the president, any two directors, or the committee." *Carried over:* hours and the v1 lead are still placeholders (checklist 1, 8).

**5. The Executive Director design conflicts with the sponsor's rules, the budget and the plan's own hiring gate.**
*Where:* Budget, Assumptions: "0% in 2027 while everyone is a contractor"; "Executive Director (fractional contract, about 60% time) $60,000"; plan, "Five priorities for the next 90 days": "Post the Executive Director role and begin interviews," beside "no role is posted until nine months of its cost is in hand"; Assumptions: "Adopted as the planning basis by the president."
*Why:* The sponsor's handbook: "Project Directors, regardless of their background/credentials cannot be Independent Contractors for their own project," a role "similar to an Executive Director role," and core work "should be done by employees" ([source](https://socialgoodfund.gitbook.io/project-handbook/paying-people-to-work-with-your-project/independent-contractors.md)); California's ABC test points the same way (Labor Code §2775, to verify). So the ED, and probably the engineer, are employees with payroll load, and 2027 personnel is understated by about a quarter of $110K. Posting now contradicts the nine-months rule, and there is no board to choose. A founder-set pay path "ratified" by the board inverts the promised process: the IRS presumption needs a conflict-free body, comparability data (three comparables suffice under $1M of receipts) and minutes within 60 days (26 CFR 53.4958-6, [text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/53.4958-6)); while sponsored, the applicable organization is the sponsor (to verify how it delegates).
*Fix:* Budget: employee lines with the 25% load in 2027, or sponsor employees at its rates. Priority 2: "Design the ED role, appoint a search committee of two independent directors and the chair, build the comparables file; post only when nine months of the role's cost is received." "Shared assumptions": "ED pay figures are planning assumptions, not offers. Each year the independent directors, founders absent, set pay against a file of at least three comparable Bay Area research nonprofits of similar budget, with the sponsor's approval while sponsored, minuted within 60 days." Keep "rising to $200K by 2031" out of anything a candidate ED sees.

**6. The conflicts policy has no rule for the seat most likely to create one.**
*Where:* Plan: "Board seats we are filling: ... AI industry"; "Independence safeguards": "15% ... from any single AI company ... 40% ... industry-pooled funds."
*Why:* The committee bars "a current employee of a lab whose models CEI evaluates"; the board does not, yet recruits an AI-industry director who may work at or invest in a lab whose results the board sees under embargo. "AI company" is undefined; "annual revenue" is unknown until year end, so a gift can breach retroactively with no remedy; 15% plus 40% allows 55% industry money; volunteers' employer matching gifts are unmentioned.
*Fix (add):* "No director may be a current employee, officer, director or more-than-nominal investor in a company that develops frontier models or whose models CEI evaluates; one who becomes so resigns or takes leave. Directors disclose ties to funders, labs and partners annually and recuse from related decisions and from pre-release access to that lab's results. 'AI company' includes affiliates, employee matching gifts and in-kind support at fair value. Caps are tested against final revenue; a breaching gift is returned, reduced or deferred, and disclosed. Industry money of all kinds is capped at 40% in total."

**7. The Tradition Partner Charter's veto, withdrawal and amendment rights work against the committee and the results promise.**
*Where:* TPC §4: "Veto over method changes ... scoring rules, item rotation and the choice of AI judges"; "Withdrawal on 60 days' notice ... unreleased test items are returned or destroyed"; §8: "amended by the board and the Methods Committee together." Charter §1(c) gives the committee "the judge–human agreement thresholds and item-retention rules."
*Why:* Dissent-published-unedited and withdrawal are wise; keep them. But veto and committee authority cover the same ground with no rule for a clash. Destroying items on withdrawal can erase the private split behind results in embargo, letting a pressured partner do what §6 forbids. Joint amendment gives a non-director body a veto with no tiebreaker. And the charter is a policy; its rights bind only through a signed module agreement.
*Fix:* Veto bullet, add: "Where the Methods Committee requires a change for validity and the partner vetoes it, the module is frozen at its last approved version and labeled; neither body overrides the other." Withdrawal bullet, add: "Withdrawal does not affect results already computed or in embargo; items used in a published or embargoed run are kept by CEI's named custodians under confidentiality, for reproducibility only." §8, replace: "Amended by the board after consulting the committee and current partners; an amendment the committee objects to in writing needs two-thirds of the directors in office. Each partner's rights are written into its module agreement, a contract. CEI may suspend or end a module agreement for breach of confidentiality or an undisclosed conflict, with the module frozen and labeled and appeal to the committee."

**8. Mechanics in the bylaws and both charters leave the first independents exposed.**
*Where:* Plan, "How the board works" (no quorum or removal rule); Charter §5: "a majority of voting members is a quorum. Decisions by majority of those present"; §4: "by a board vote."
*Why:* With no members, §5222 lets "a majority of the directors then in office" remove a director without cause ([text](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_corp_code_section_5222); verify (b) and (f)). The chair is seated in Q4 2026 and the cohort in Q1 2027, so for a quarter three insiders can remove the chair. On the committee, two votes at a quorum of three can block a $30K to $40K module.
*Fix:* Bylaws: "Seven to nine directors; quorum a majority of directors in office; removal without cause by two-thirds of directors in office; three-year terms in three classes after the one-year first term; at most two consecutive." Seat the chair and at least three independents at one meeting. Charter §5: "Blocking a module, declining a specification, or changing inclusion criteria needs a majority of all voting members." §4: "removed by two-thirds of the directors in office after the member is heard and the committee consulted; the first cohort draws one-, two- and three-year terms by lot."

## 3. Should fix

1. **Fee is likely 8%, not 6%.** Budget and plan: "6% ... to be confirmed." The sponsor's published Model A rate is 8% to $500K, 7% to $1M; domestic Model C is 6.5% ([fee page](https://socialgoodfund.gitbook.io/project-handbook/social-good-fund-sponsorship-admin-cost.md)): about $6K more in 2027, $12K in 2028. Verify against the executed agreement. *Carried over.*
2. **"Own 501(c)(3) from 2029" is a date, not a plan.** The sponsor transfers assets only to a holder of an IRS determination letter ([termination page](https://socialgoodfund.gitbook.io/project-handbook/terminating-your-sponsorship-agreement.md)) and is silent on IP and employees. Add a Form 1023 filing date (within 27 months of formation if retroactivity matters, to verify), an exit agreement covering IP, staff and grant re-papering, Attorney General registration within 30 days of first holding assets (Gov. Code §12585, to verify), and a fee that runs until the letter arrives.
3. **"Christian-led" needs counsel's sentence.** The Articles' purpose is secular and the entity is a public benefit corporation. Confirm a faith qualification for directors is lawful in the bylaws, check the nondiscrimination terms of the Humanity AI funders listed, and define "practicing" as self-described. *Carried over* (checklist 4).
4. **Committee honoraria** are promised (Charter §8) and absent from budget v3. Add about $10K or write "unpaid in 2027." *Carried over* (checklist 11).
5. **Legal at $3K** cannot buy bylaws, a conflicts policy, the sponsorship amendment, 40 contributor licenses, module agreements and a lab pre-release agreement. Budget $10K to $15K or name the pro bono source.
6. **"Neither body reviews results before publication" (Charter §7)** needs a narrow carve-out for legal review of a report's text (defamation, breach of a pre-release agreement): numbers never change, any edit logged in the report.
7. **The clocks disagree:** brief, "Months 1–3 (from first-tranche funding): seat the board chair and founding cohort" versus the plan's Q4 2026 and Q1 2027.
8. **Drop the executive committee** ("once the board passes six members"); a seven-member board needs a finance and audit committee and a governance committee.
9. **State the officers now.** Section 5213 already requires a secretary and treasurer who are not the president or chair; "named at the first meeting" implies the filing is incomplete.
10. **From 2029, Gov. Code §12586(g)** requires the board or a committee to find CEO and CFO pay "just and reasonable" at hiring and each change (to verify applicability); calendar it now.

## 5. What is strong

- "Still open, stated plainly." Keep it, and keep it last.
- Protections that cost the founders something: $0 founder pay in writing, an executive session without founders at every meeting, the one-year first term, and the charter's "What the committee does not decide."
- Independence as numbers with an embargo rule and no badges; the Oct 27 loopholes (in-kind at fair value, any-size review of lab gifts, director recusal) are closed.
- The dated funding tiers, the nine-months rule, and two budget paths both "within the money raised."
- The partner's right to publish dissent unedited, and withdrawal that leaves open-licensed items available.
