# Nonprofit-CFO review: CEI Budget v3 and the financial sections of the plan set

Read Oct 31, 2026 against `plan/CEI-Budget-v3-2027-2031.xlsx` (values and formulas, recomputed independently), `plan/BUSINESS-PLAN-2027-2029.md`, `plan/BOARD-BRIEF.md`, the budget lines of `plan/TECHNICAL-BRIEF.md` and `plan/CHRISTIAN-EDITION.md`, the Oct 27 reviews, `plan/INTERNAL-CHECKLIST.md` and `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`. The Oct 28 decisions are taken as adopted. Cell references are `Sheet!Cell`. Anything I could not confirm is marked "to verify".

## 1. Verdict

Not yet, for one reason that decides it: the workbook is built on the sponsor's rules as the plan hopes they are, not as Social Good Fund publishes them. Its handbook prices Model A at 8% of all receipts (6.5% for domestic Model C), says it does not advance money, requires approval for anyone paid $2,000 or more as a contractor, warns against stipends for work, and suggests budgeting about 13.5% for payroll taxes alone. Applied to `Aggressive!B18`, `B32` and `B34:B36`, those rules turn the 2027 "set aside for reserves" of $9,820 into a deficit of roughly $13K to $27K before any grant arrives late. Everything else is well built: every subtotal, total and reserve line recomputes to the cent, the plan's 2027 table ties to the workbook total exactly, and the driver logic is sound. Fix the sponsor-and-cash items (must-fix 1 to 5), add the rows a finance committee reads first (burn, runway, surplus, overhead), and I would sign it for a board packet as a planning basis.

## 2. Must fix before this goes to a board or funder

**1. The sponsor fee rate and base are wrong.** `Assumptions!B5:C5` = 6%, note H5 "6% under the Social Good Fund agreement (rate to be confirmed)". Social Good Fund's published schedule is [8% for Model A, 6.5% for domestic Model C](https://socialgoodfund.gitbook.io/project-handbook/social-good-fund-sponsorship-admin-cost), charged on total receipts for the month, stepping down 1% per $500K of calendar-year revenue to a 3% floor, plus a $29 monthly cost. OPEN-QUESTIONS §1 says Model A was chosen in June. *Why:* at 8% the 2027 fee is $24,640, not $18,480; 2027-2028 fees rise about $22K on the aggressive path and $13K on the conservative one, and the fee is 2.6x the 2027 reserve. *Fix:* add a Model A/C input on Assumptions; set `B5` to 8% (or 6.5%) until the executed agreement says otherwise; model the step-down for 2028 (`=MIN(rev,500000)*8%+MAX(rev-500000,0)*7%`, to verify the mechanics); add the $29/month to `Aggressive!B61`. Replace "6%" in the plan's 2027 table, "Shared assumptions", the board brief and `Changes vs v2!C17`.

**2. The 0% payroll load in 2027 rests on classifications that will not hold.** `Assumptions!B6` = 0 ("0% in 2027 while everyone is a contractor"); `Aggressive!B34:B36` carry a 60%-time Executive Director, a half-time engineer and a co-lead "stipend" with no load. Under Labor Code § 2775 a worker is an employee unless all three ABC prongs are met, and prong B requires work "outside the usual course of the hiring entity's business" ([DIR FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_independentcontractor.htm)); a compensated officer running the organization and the engineer building its only product cannot pass it ([nonprofit law blog](https://nonprofitlawblog.com/is-a-compensated-nonprofit-officer-an-employee/)). Under Model A the sponsor is the employer: its handbook requires approval before anyone earning $2,000+ is treated as a contractor, says [not to use a stipend when someone is being paid for work](https://socialgoodfund.gitbook.io/project-handbook/accounts-payable-outgoing-money/stipends-and-honorariums), and suggests [about 13.5% for payroll taxes](https://socialgoodfund.gitbook.io/project-handbook/paying-people-to-work-with-your-project/independent-contractors) before benefits. *Why:* 13.5% on the $122K of 2027 contractor pay is $16.5K; the workbook's own 25% is $30.5K; with must-fix 1 the 2027 aggressive year closes $13K to $27K short. *Fix:* add a row `=SUM(B34:B36)*Assumptions!B$6` with `B6` at 13.5% (taxes only) or 25% (with benefits); relabel the co-lead "part-time employee"; decide what gives (defer the convening or half the annotation to the second-tranche close, or raise the ask to about $335K); update `Changes vs v2!C18`.

**3. Cash timing inside 2027 is missing, and the sponsor does not advance money.** The scenario sheets are annual; the plan says "foundations pay six to twelve months after first contact" and "no 2027 funding committed", and the sponsor's handbook says [it does not advance funds and the project must keep enough in its account to cover payroll](https://socialgoodfund.gitbook.io/project-handbook/paying-people-to-work-with-your-project/employees). *Why:* spending is modeled from January; $17K on hand covers one month at tranche-1 burn; second-half 2027 burn is about $31K a month (tranche-1 lines plus the fellow, annotation and convening), not the plan's $23K average. *Fix:* add a "2027 cash" sheet: opening balance (the $17K less 2026 spend), receipts by quarter by source tagged committed / likely / pipeline, disbursements by quarter by line, closing balance, and the hire triggers ("nine months in hand") as formulas. Quarterly is enough; monthly once a hire date exists.

**4. The reserve is a plug, measured on the wrong year, and partly unfundable.** `Aggressive!B69` `=B19-B66` ("what is left after spending"); `B71` `=B66/12*3` tests the target against the same year's spending; `B76` `=B66+B69` equals `B19` by construction. *Why:* (a) a residual is not a policy: any shortfall takes it first, and nothing is left in 2027 once must-fix 1-2 land; (b) with spending growing 60-160% a year the reserve must cover next year: measured forward, end-2029 holds 1.9 months (aggressive) and 1.8 (conservative), end-2030 2.0 and 2.2, so "both reach the three-month target in 2029" is true only on a trailing basis; (c) restricted project grants cannot be set aside as reserve, so the "first reserve contribution" inside tranche 2 is fundable only from unrestricted gifts; (d) under Model A the reserve sits in the sponsor's fund and leaves on exit only with its consent and funders' (exit terms are "to confirm"). *Fix:* make the contribution an input row (e.g. 3% of unrestricted revenue) and add "Unallocated surplus/(deficit)" `=B19-B66-B69` as the check; set `B71` to `=C66/12*Assumptions!$B$10` (next year's spending; the last column uses its own); add a forward months row; in the plan replace "Both reach a three-month operating reserve in 2029" with "two months of the following year's budget by 2030, three by 2031, funded from unrestricted gifts".

**5. There is no burn or runway line anywhere in the workbook.** "Months of cushion held" (`B72`) is a reserve ratio, not runway. *Why:* the first finance-committee question is "what is our runway on January 1, 2028?"; the answer in the model is $9,820 against $54.5K a month of 2028 spending: five days. The plan's "Tranche 1 alone ... funds 12 months" is circular (tranche 1 is sized to tranche-1 lines). *Fix:* add rows: "Monthly spending next year" `=C66/12`, "Runway at year end (months)" `=B70/(C66/12)`, and "Burn, second half of 2027" on the cash sheet; carry all three to the Summary; rewrite the plan's "Burn and runway" paragraph with the first-half, second-half and January 2028 figures.

**6. Revenue is one undifferentiated input and neither path is a downside.** `Conservative!B5:F5` grows 500%, 117%, 69%, 59%; `Aggressive` 160%, 125%, 67%, 67%. Both assume Templeton- or Lilly-scale awards decided before v1 exists; the paths differ by only 25% in 2028; and the 15%/40% independence caps cannot be checked without a source split. *Why:* a committee will ask "what if we raise $400K in 2028, not $800K", and the workbook cannot answer; the board-chair review asked for a probability-weighted table and the documents still lack one. *Fix:* add a revenue block on each scenario sheet: the plan's four channels plus board and major donors, each tagged restricted/unrestricted and committed/likely/pipeline, summing to row 5; add a "Floor" case (tier-2 2027, then $300-400K flat) or a sensitivity row showing which lines are cut at 75% and 50% of plan. Rename "Conservative" to "Slower start" unless a true floor is added.

**7. Overhead is invisible, and some of it is not fundable from project grants.** The fee sits above the line as a revenue deduction (`B18`), so "Administration" reads 8.7% of 2027 spending when fee plus admin is 13.9% of gross (15.9% at 8%); `B62` mixes fundraising, unallowable on most project grants, with program travel. Templeton caps indirect costs at about 15% ([secondary source](https://www.cmu.edu/engage/partner/foundations/faculty-staff/overhead-policies/foundation-relations-administrative-fee-information1.pdf); confirm at templeton.org, to verify) and sponsor fees usually count against such caps. *Fix:* show the fee as an administration line in a functional view (program / management / fundraising) with an "Effective overhead rate" row `=(fee+admin)/gross`; split fundraising from travel; in the plan's "Committed vs. pipeline", say that the $50K+ from board and major donors pays the fee, administration and reserve ($52.5K in 2027 at current rates) while foundation grants pay program.

**8. The v1 modules cost $8K each; every later module costs $30K.** `Aggressive!B45` = $40,000 for five modules against `Assumptions!B12` = $30,000 (and $4K per module in `Conservative!B45`); the Christian edition's Give ladder says "$20K-$40K sponsors a tradition's scholars". *Why:* the Oct 27 faith-funder review already said $40K across four modules "assumes theologians who work like crowd raters"; the workbook now prices that assumption into the 2027 ask beside a unit cost four times higher, and costing v1 at the unit rate adds about $110K to 2027. *Fix:* either re-cost 2027 or add to `Assumptions!H12` and the plan's "Unit costs": "v1 modules are authored by volunteer scholars already working with CEI, with $8K of honoraria and gold-set rating each; the $30K rate applies to modules authored by a new tradition partner from 2028." Make the Christian edition's $20K-$40K line refer to post-v1 modules.

## 3. Should fix

1. **Contingency is zero in the two thinnest years** (`Assumptions!B7:C7`, adopted Oct 28). The decision creates a problem: 2027 aggressive slack is 3.5% of spending and vanishes under must-fix 1-2, while 2029-2031 carry both a 5% contingency and a 3-month reserve for the same risk. Keep the decision but add a "cut order" row for 2027-2028 (convening, then annotation timing, then the fellow's start) so the board sees the contingency plan in place of a contingency line. Contingency inside TOTAL SPENDING also inflates the reserve target by 5%.
2. **Multilingual formula re-pays every translation every year.** `Aggressive!F51` `=F6*F10*Assumptions!$B$14` charges 14 modules x 3 languages in 2031 ($420K). If translation is one-time per module-language pair, 2031 is overstated by $220K and five years by about $300K (conservative: $80K). Use new pairs `=(F6*F10-E6*E10)*rate` plus a per-pair maintenance rate; if the line is meant to include annual non-English runs, say so in H51.
3. **Maintenance is charged on modules built the same year.** `C47` `=C6*Assumptions!$B$13` includes the modules in `C7`; H47 says new modules are excluded in 2027 only. Use `=(C6-C7)*Assumptions!$B$13` (about $45K over five years aggressive, $30K conservative).
4. **An independent audit from 2030.** Once independent, California requires audited statements and an audit committee at $2M gross revenue ([Gov. Code § 12586(e)](https://www.oag.ca.gov/sites/all/files/agweb/pdfs/charities/pdf/nonprofit-integrity-act-nov04-auditreq.pdf)); both paths cross it in 2030 and funders often ask sooner. `E58` = $26K cannot hold an audit plus bookkeeping: plan $40-50K from 2030, add an audit committee to the governance text, and put one-time 2029 transition costs (payroll, HR, registrations, own insurance) in `D58:D59`. A partial-year fee in 2029 is more realistic than zero.
5. **The hosted fellow at $75K "with the host's overhead inside the figure"** (`H37`). State stipend, benefits rate and host overhead separately; at a 50% university rate the stipend falls below postdoc scale (to verify with the host). If the fellow is not hosted, the 25% load applies (+$9.4K in 2027). The sponsor's handbook notes a funder paying a cost directly avoids the fee: a grant paid to the host university saves the fee on that line.
6. **The compute unit cost needs the pilot's number.** `Assumptions!H11` assumes 5,000-item modules at 2K tokens an item; the technical brief describes gold subsets of 300-500 items, paraphrase and format variants, two judge vendors and cue/no-cue arms, and long reasoning outputs multiply tokens. $1,200 may be generous for v1 and thin for 2031's $1.0M line (`Aggressive!F44`, 23% of spending). Add a "variants per item" driver, re-base on the brief's promised "dry-run cost per model" once the pilot runs, and model researcher credits as an offset row.
7. **Recruiting fees** remain unbudgeted (the plan says so); a full-time ED search in 2028 is typically $20-30K. Add to `C62` on both paths.
8. **Conservative 2027 banks 16% of revenue** (`Conservative!B69` = $16,100) while the co-lead stipend, "the reviewers' first condition", is unfunded (`B36` = 0). Either fund $12K or say why reserve comes first in the plan's tier text.
9. **The 25% load is defensible; say what it buys.** Employer taxes on a $120K salary run about 8-9%; BLS puts benefits at [about 30% of total compensation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.nr0.htm) including paid leave already inside salary, so an add-on load of 20-28% is the range depending on family coverage. Add to H6: "assumes employee-only health coverage and a 3-4% retirement match; each 5 points is about $12K in 2028 and $57K in 2031 (aggressive)". Workers' comp appears in `A57` and inside the 25%: pick one place.
10. **Layout for a finance committee.** `Summary!H4` and `H21` promise "Basis and notes" and the column is empty; add net revenue, surplus/deficit, runway, overhead % and personnel % rows; the legend says yellow marks adopted policy numbers but only `Assumptions!B10` is yellow; all sheets print portrait with fit-to-page off (set landscape, fit to one page wide, repeat row 4). Confirm what `B57` buys under Model A: the sponsor carries general liability, workers' comp and D&O for its own directors, not the project's.

## 5. What is strong

- Formula hygiene: zero hard-coded totals in rows 16-76, inputs in blue, unit costs on one sheet, a Summary that is 100% links. My independent recomputation matched every subtotal, total, reserve and cushion line on both paths to the cent. Editors should keep that discipline: never type over a black cell.
- The plan's 2027 table reconciles to the workbook total exactly, and every other dollar figure across the four documents ties. That consistency is rare and worth protecting.
- Driver-based research costs (modules x models x runs; count x unit cost) let a committee flex one number and see the five-year effect, and make the two paths honestly comparable.
- The change log, the explicit $0 founder-compensation row, and the "What each path achieves" row that ties money to milestones.
- The dated funding tiers and the nine-months-in-hand rule are the right policy; the cash sheet only needs to show them operating.
