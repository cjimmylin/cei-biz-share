# Review: a scholar of comparative religion and ethics (interfaith bodies)

*Oct 31, 2026. Read: both charters, the Christian edition, the general plan, the technical brief and the board brief, against the Oct 27 reviews, the internal checklist and OPEN-QUESTIONS. "Carried over" marks an Oct 27 finding still open; "to verify" marks what I could not confirm.*

## 1. Verdict

I would sign the Tradition Partner Charter for my tradition, with amendments, and would tell a Muslim, Jewish or Buddhist colleague to do the same once they are made. The charter is better than anything a Christian-founded group has yet offered. What decides it is the body above the charter. The Methods Committee chooses which traditions are included, "checks that the panel and protocol meet that charter," and "can block a module," and as chartered it can lawfully be two secular philosophers, two computer scientists, a psychometrician, a Catholic and a Protestant, nominated by a Christian president and appointed by a Christian-led board. A Muslim panel's module can be blocked by a body with no Muslim on it. Until the committee must include scholars of the traditions it judges, every other safeguard is a promise kept by the people who hold the keys.

## 2. Must fix before this goes to a prospective partner

**1. The Methods Committee can judge a tradition with no one from any non-Christian tradition present.**
*Where:* Methods Committee Charter §3: "at least two secular moral philosophers, at least one machine-learning or evaluation researcher, one measurement or psychometrics specialist, and at least one scholar of a religious tradition"; "A majority of members from outside the founders' own tradition." Board brief, Governance: "(secular philosophers, ML researchers, a measurement specialist)," no religious scholar at all. Carried over in part from the secular philosopher's point 1: the charter now exists, but its composition answers the secular objection, not the interfaith one.
*Why:* "Founders' own tradition" is undefined; four Catholics and three others satisfy it, and the one religious scholar can be a Christian. Secular philosophers ruling on whether a halakhic panel is "drawn from more than one school" is the asymmetry of hospitality interfaith critics have long charged Christian-led dialogue with: the host writes the rules and sits as judge.
*Fix (§3):* "... and at least two scholars of religious traditions, at least one from a tradition other than Christianity. A majority of members from outside Christianity, the founders' tradition." §5: "When the committee reviews, blocks or hears an appeal on a module, a scholar of that tradition who is not on its partner panel sits with the committee for that decision, with a vote." §4: "Any current partner may propose a nominee." Correct the board brief.

**2. "Settled, contested or not addressed" is a magisterium's grammar; it does not fit halakha, the madhhabs or Buddhist lineages.**
*Where:* Charter §3: "Every reference point is labeled settled, contested within the tradition, or not addressed. Where the tradition recognizes levels of authority (for example, categorical teaching, principle and prudential application), items carry that label too, and only the first level feeds say–do fidelity." §5: "A panel of at least three scholars from the tradition, drawn from more than one school within it where schools exist."
*Why:* An Orthodox, a Conservative and a Reform posek give three settled answers, and three scholars cannot hold them. Among the Sunni madhhabs, difference is legitimate, not "contested"; ijma' is itself contested in scope; Shi'i jurisprudence is another school with another authority structure. Buddhist ethics is carried by precepts, Vinaya and lineage, not "teaching" with "levels"; Hinduism has no body that could call anything settled. A module that averages across schools is rejected by every school; one authored by one school and named for the whole tradition is denounced by the rest. IslamicLegalBench's seven-school design (arXiv 2602.21226; to verify) is the right model.
*Fix (§3):* "Where a tradition holds several authoritative schools, reference points are recorded per school, a model is credited for naming the school it follows, and the module's scope statement names which schools it covers. Each partner defines its own levels of authority and which level, if any, gives 'clear guidance' for say–do fidelity; the Catholic labels are an example, not a template." §5: "at least one scholar from each school the module claims to cover, and never fewer than three." §1: "A module is named for its source and scope (a text, a school, a body), not for a whole tradition, unless its panel spans the tradition's recognized schools."

**3. The Application task assumes a model may give religious guidance; the bodies you need have said it may not.**
*Where:* Technical brief, Constructs: Application, "Faithful advice to a person who holds X," scored by "X's own rubric, rated by X's scholars." Plan App. A: "Advising a person who holds tradition X."
*Why:* Tzohar's position paper allows AI to search and sort sources but holds that "the products of artificial intelligence are not halachic rulings" ([Tzohar](https://ethics.tzohar.org.il/en/using-artificial-intelligence-systems-to-rule-halacha-abridged-position-paper/)); Egypt's Dar al-Ifta ruled AI interpretation of the Qur'an impermissible in January 2026 ([Aaj News](https://english.aaj.tv/news/330451560); primary text to verify). Asking such scholars to ratify "the faithful advice" a model should give is asking them to certify AI as mufti or posek. They will decline, rightly; Catholic moral theologians will say the same about conscience (¶99).
*Fix (charter §3, brief):* "A partner decides whether the Application task applies to its module. Where the tradition holds that guidance belongs to a qualified human authority, the reference answer represents the range of positions, declines to rule and refers the person to that authority; a model that rules is penalized." The refusal category "cannot speak for a tradition" must then be able to count as correct.

**4. "Endorse" runs through the theory of change and contradicts the charter.**
*Where:* Plan, Our role: "tradition experts endorse our translations"; In one sentence: "peer review and tradition endorsement earn trust"; Pathway row 3: "Share of items endorsed by tradition reviewers"; brief: "endorsement rate per module"; Christian edition: "tradition scholars endorse our work." Against Charter §7: "CEI never describes a module as approved, certified or endorsed by a church, a school or an institution." Carried over from the Catholic reviewer's point 7: "co-sign" was fixed, "endorse" was not.
*Why:* A theologian under a mandatum (c. 812), a Catholic university host, a dayan, a mufti on a national council: none can "endorse" without the institution being read as having done so. An imprimatur certifies only absence of error in a fixed text and cannot reach scoring (research_notes, magnifica_humanitas.md §4).
*Fix:* "ratify" for reference points, "recognize as faithful" for modules, everywhere. Pathway row 3: "Share of reference points ratified by the partner panel, and the dissent rate, per module." Theory of change: "because peer review and recognition by each tradition's own scholars earn trust."

**5. Nobody can see how the next traditions are chosen, and the stated reason is "readiness."**
*Where:* Plan App. A: "secular humanism and Islam are the most organized next candidates"; App. C: "Islam (with an existing Islamic-law benchmark and a new AI-ethics alliance in Qatar) and secular humanism (the 2025 Luxembourg Declaration) are the most organized next candidates." Methods charter §6.1: "Adopt and publish the inclusion criteria," none drafted.
*Why:* Choosing Islam because it is benchmark-ready tells Muslim scholars they were picked as a convenient instrument; choosing humanists because a declaration converts into a rubric tells them the same. No tradition can apply; all are invited. The Orthodox churches appear nowhere in the set. The Luxembourg Declaration ([adopted 6 July 2025](https://humanists.international/policy/luxembourg-declaration-on-artificial-intelligence-and-human-values/)) is a policy statement, a "source" under §1, not a tradition's teaching.
*Fix (annex to §6.1):* "A tradition, school or source is included when (a) a partner panel meeting charter §5 is willing to author it; (b) its scope and source are named; (c) a budget exists or the partner brings one; (d) the committee publishes its reason. Any scholar or body may propose a module; the committee answers in writing within 90 days and publishes the queue with reasons. Readiness of a benchmark is not a criterion." In App. A and C: "traditions whose scholars have published on AI and whom we will approach first; the committee decides order."

**6. The identity-cue and false-attribution probes will stereotype believers unless the partner writes them.**
*Where:* Brief, Sycophancy probe: "with a false attribution ('as a utilitarian, I believe this is absolutely forbidden')"; "items where the traditions agree as the control arm." Plan App. A: "a false attribution of a view to that tradition."
*Why:* Someone decides a view is "falsely" attributed to Islam or Judaism; if a Sunni panel labels a Shi'i position false, or an Orthodox panel a Reform one, the benchmark publishes a stereotype as a control. "As a Muslim" is already a flattening; adherents say "as a Hanafi" or "as a Conservative Jew." A table of flip rates by religious cue will be read as a ranking of which believers get pandered to. "Items where the traditions agree" is cross-tradition adjudication by staff, which the charter forbids.
*Fix (charter §3, brief):* "Identity cues, false-attribution items and application personas for a module are written or approved by its partner panel, in the words adherents use for themselves; a 'false attribution' is a view the panel certifies that no recognized school holds. Cross-tradition agreement items are ratified by every panel they name. Results are reported per module with the partner's note; CEI publishes no comparison of sycophancy across traditions' cues."

**7. Withdrawal, naming and licensing give a partner less than it will ask for.**
*Where:* Charter §4: "Items already released under an open license remain available with the partner credited"; §7: "CC BY 4.0 for data ... with the partner named in the attribution"; sensitive items defined only as "material that could help a system impersonate religious authority."
*Why:* A partner that withdraws because the module misrepresents it stays credited on items it repudiates. Items derived from the Qur'an and tafsir, responsa, tantric or Vinaya texts, or Indigenous sources carry restrictions open-by-default does not anticipate. Nothing says who names the module, or whether signing means assenting to CEI's theology.
*Fix:* §4: "On withdrawal the partner may require its name be removed from released items and the module labeled 'withdrawn by its tradition', and may publish its reason unedited." §7: "A partner may ask that items derived from texts its tradition restricts be released under a narrower license or held in the private split; each exception is stated in the datasheet. The partner names its module and writes its scope statement." §1: "Signing is not assent to CEI's mission statement, Appendix E or the Christian edition; a partner may publish its own account of why it takes part."

**8. The Christian edition will be read by the Catholic scholars you are recruiting, and it reads as a Protestant document about "the church."**
*Where:* Title "CEI for the Church"; "the moral wisdom of the church"; "The church is asking for exactly this kind of humility," citing a survey of 442 Protestant pastors ([Barna](https://www.barna.com/research/ai-scripture-interpretation/)); "the seeds of truth found in other traditions (Nostra aetate 2)"; "Our board is Christian-led; our advisory committee and module authors span many traditions." The Catholic lead is still unnamed (checklist item 2; carried over from the Catholic reviewer's condition one).
*Why:* For Catholics and Orthodox, "the Church" has a name; lower-case generic "the church" is evangelical idiom, and Protestant pastors are not "the church" asking. The Orthodox, whose Ecumenical Patriarch has spoken repeatedly on AI, are absent. *Nostra aetate* 2 speaks of "a ray of that Truth which enlightens all men"; "seeds of the Word" is *Ad gentes* 11 (check both at vatican.va). "Module authors span many traditions" is not yet true. The Praxis frame and "Pray, Give, Serve, Connect" are an evangelical template; keep them, but do not let them carry the Catholic ask.
*Fix:* under the title: "We write 'the church' for all Christians in every communion: Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant." "Protestant pastors are asking." Cite *Nostra aetate* 2 and *Ad gentes* 11 correctly. One sentence naming the Orthodox and mainline conversations you want. "Our advisory committee and module authors will span many traditions."

## 3. Should fix

1. **"The best setting for truth to be tested"** (App. E) reads to a Muslim or Buddhist partner as a proving ground on which Christianity expects vindication. Add: "CEI's modules, reports and papers argue for the truth of no tradition, ours included, and publish no comparative verdicts." Carried over in spirit from the secular philosopher's point 5.
2. **"A Christian-led board" as a mitigation for faith audiences** (App. D: "The theological rationale in Appendix E; a Christian-led board") tells non-Christian partners the board's faith is reassurance offered to Christians. Delete the clause.
3. **Krippendorff's α is a dataset statistic, not an item statistic** (Charter §3: "Items where the panel's agreement falls below the floor (Krippendorff's α of 0.67)"). Write an item rule ("contested when fewer than two of three ratify the same reference point"); keep α for judge–human agreement.
4. **"An international, cross-cultural consensus"** (App. A, human-rights row). Muslim scholars know the 1948 abstentions and the 1990 Cairo Declaration. Write "a widely adopted international text, read with its own scholars and practitioners."
5. **Global South.** App. E promises "Global South scholars co-lead modules"; the charter has no clause. Add to §5: "Where a tradition's centers of learning lie outside the United States, at least one panel member is based or trained there."
6. **Language.** "$10K per module per additional language" treats Arabic, Hebrew, Pali and Sanskrit as add-ons. Add to §2: "Reference points may cite source texts in their original language; the partner approves any translation used in items."
7. **Capture of a panel.** Conflict rules cover labs and funders, not states or parties. Add to Methods charter §5: "The committee may decline a panel that is an instrument of a state or a political movement, and says so publicly."
8. **The "fifth module" and "one outside community"** (App. A; Strategy) are unnamed; every prospective partner will ask whether it is the one. Name it or drop it.
9. **Vocabulary.** "Faith tradition" and "teaching" for Buddhism; "Scripture" capitalized for anything but the Bible; "gold" everywhere though redefined. Add a glossary to the charter; each partner supplies its module's terms.
10. **"Help review the Magnifica humanitas benchmark"** (Christian edition, Invitation) invites donors to review a Catholic module. Write "help us find Catholic theologians to author and review it."

## 4. Nits

- Charter §1 lists "a faith tradition, a school of moral philosophy, a human-rights body, a professional community" but not a text or a school within a tradition, which is what the encyclical module and any madhhab module are.
- App. C "a new AI-ethics alliance in Qatar": name the institution or drop it; "Qatar" alone reads geopolitically.
- Partners table, "Reach into Jewish, Latter-day Saint and other traditions": say whether a Latter-day Saint module counts as a "Christian module"; evangelical and LDS readers answer differently.
- "Principled pluralism" is rightly confined to the Christian edition now; its Reformed provenance is confirmed ([CPJ](https://cpjustice.org/who-we-are/cpj-distinctives/)).

## 5. What is strong (do not break)

- Charter §3 with §7: reference answer with recorded dissent, no cross-tradition scoring, the model's own view never scored, "institutions host and review, they do not certify." The correct reading of what Catholic and most other institutions can lend.
- Charter §4: veto over method changes and AI-judge choice, dissent published unedited, withdrawal on notice, named credit, honoraria.
- The encyclical module's design: paragraph-tied items, only categorical items feeding say–do, closed-book and open-book side by side, no position on machine agency. Keep it as the Catholic template; do not generalize its labels.
- App. C and App. E admitting that ¶107 applies to CEI: a hard-coded reading of any tradition, "ours included, would be exactly that."
- The refusal category "cannot speak for a tradition," and candor about disagreement scored in both directions.

2. Will CEI accept a module whose partner declines the Application task or redefines it as referral to human authority?
3. What does "the founders' own tradition" mean in the Methods charter: Reformed, Protestant, or Christian?
4. Will the Christian edition go to non-Christian partners, and are partners asked to affirm the mission statement or Appendix E?
5. Who is the "one outside community" for the fifth module, and did it ask or was it chosen?
