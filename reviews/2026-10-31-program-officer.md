# Program-officer review: the CEI plan set

*Stance: a program officer who has worked at a faith-based foundation and at an AI-safety funder. Read Oct 31, 2026, against the Oct 27 reviews, INTERNAL-CHECKLIST.md and OPEN-QUESTIONS.md. "Carried over" marks an Oct 27 finding still open; "to verify" marks what I could not settle, with where to check.*

## 1. Verdict

I would not recommend the $202K first tranche as written, and no funder I have worked for could pay it by the plan's own March 31, 2027 deadline. I would recommend a project grant of $60K–$100K for a pre-registered say–do and sycophancy pilot on two modules, conditional on one thing: a named person, with hours, accountable for shipping it. The research design is better than most proposals in this space and the honesty is rare; the organization behind it has no accountable lead, no technical co-lead, no Catholic co-lead for its only faith module, no settled legal structure, and a funder list whose program pages, checked today, fit neither the ask nor the calendar. The thing that most decides it: whether "Benchmark v1 is led by [name], [N] hours a week" becomes a sentence with a name in it.

## 2. Must fix before this goes to a funder

**1. Three named-person gaps, and the first dollar buys the wrong role.** *Carried over.*
*General plan, Team and governance:* "Benchmark v1 is led by [name], [N] hours a week." *Partners we still need:* "Catholic theologians to write and review the encyclical module." *Board brief, Who we are:* the v1 lead and technical co-lead "are not yet named."
*Why:* a placeholder in the sentence that answers "who does the work" ends the read. And the plan contradicts itself on what comes first: *Executive summary:* "First, about $202K — an Executive Director, a half-time research engineer..." against *Financial plan, Shared assumptions:* "Hiring order: engineer and technical co-lead first." Research funders will not pay for an ED; neither will the faith funders named (see 2). ED-first shrinks the pool to individuals.
*Fix:* name the lead and hours before circulation; if no founder can write the hours down, remove the 12-month date. Rewrite the tranche: "First, about $202K: a technical co-lead and a research engineer, compute and infrastructure, legal and insurance, and a fractional operations lead once nine months of that cost is in hand." Present *Magnifica humanitas* as "proposed, pending a named Catholic co-lead" everywhere until one is named (INTERNAL-CHECKLIST item 2 says this; the documents still say "four benchmarks already in active development").

**3. The one grant received may be a sub-grant, and "DELTA partner" may overstate.** *New, to verify.*
*Traction:* "a $7K+ faith-and-AI rapid-response grant through Notre Dame's Lilly-funded DELTA partner program (administered by Faith Work & Tech)"; *Partners:* "A DELTA partner, with a partner grant." The source notes read "$100K rapid response grant > $7k for CEI," and Notre Dame lists FWT, not CEI, as the grantee.
*Why:* a Notre Dame or Lilly officer will know, and an overstated grant history loses a faith funder fastest.
*Fix:* confirm with FWT and DELTA, then: "a $7K sub-award from Faith Work & Tech's Notre Dame DELTA rapid-response grant (2026)," and "connected to the DELTA network through that award." Apply in the board brief and Christian edition.

**4. Half of Tranche 1 is overhead that only individuals will fund, and the plan does not show it.** *New; the named-gift point is carried over.*
*2027 budget:* ED $60K, legal/accounting/insurance $14K, IT/travel/fundraising $10K, sponsor fee $12K: $96K of $202K. *The invitation:* "Fund the Executive Director's first year, $60K."
*Why:* every funder above funds research. "Matching the ask to the funder" says the right thing; the budget never shows who pays which line, so a reader cannot see that the plan works only if individuals cover about $100K. One donor owning the ED is the governance smell the board-chair review raised.
*Fix:* a sources-and-uses table: each 2027 line, the funder type expected to pay it, status (committed, asked, not asked). Replace the ED row with "Found the institution (general operating), $60K–$100K," and drop the named-role framing in the Christian edition's "Give."

**5. The impact table measures activity.** *New.*
*Measuring impact, 12 months:* "v1 preprinted and submitted"; "at least one harness integration"; "Share of items endorsed, reported per module"; *Tracked, not required:* "Influence." The only number with a bar is the 0.67 α floor, a rule rather than a target.
*Why:* a committee asks what will be different, by how much, by when. Reporting a share is not an outcome; a target share is.
*Fix:* "By month 12: at least two modules reach α ≥ 0.67 on every reported dimension; at least 70% of gold items endorsed by the tradition panel, dissent published; judge–human agreement at or above threshold on every AI-judged dimension. By month 18: at least one organization outside CEI has run the suite and shared results; if none has, the board does not fund v2 on the same distribution model" (the plan's own rule from Strategy and roadmap, moved where a funder will see it).

**6. The scope still exceeds the capacity, and "half-time" is a quarter.** *Carried over, partly addressed.*
*Executive summary:* "four benchmarks already in active development." *Financial plan, footnote:* "The engineer line buys roughly 500 contract hours ... closer to a quarter of a full-time year than half." INTERNAL-CHECKLIST item 10 (the two-module fallback) is undecided.
*Why:* two technical reviewers judged four gold-set modules plus say–do beyond this capacity; the plan answered by making the fifth module and kit "stretch" while keeping four as the headline and a quarter-time engineer labeled "half-time" in every summary.
*Fix:* headline "v1: two modules (one secular, *Magnifica humanitas* once a Catholic co-lead is named) plus the say–do and sycophancy pilots; two further modules if Tranche 2 closes by mid-2027," and "research engineer (about 500 contract hours)" wherever "half-time" appears.

**7. The two identities cancel in the wrong documents.** *New.*
To a secular funder the general plan reads Christian: *Executive summary:* "founded by Christians who believe every person bears the image of God"; *Team:* "a weekly Sabbath, to bringing major decisions to prayer"; *The invitation, For funders:* "we will pray for you as you pray for this work"; *Appendix E:* "Christ is Lord of it." To a church donor the Christian edition reads secular: the only Christian module is a papal encyclical from Protestant founders; *What success looks like:* "Inclusion criteria are set by an independent methods committee that includes secular philosophers"; and the charter lets that committee, "a majority from outside the founders' tradition," block any module.
*Why:* SFF or Foresight reviewers will discount a plan that prays for them; a Lilly-type donor will ask why secular philosophers gate the church's module. Both readings are fair on the text.
*Fix:* in the general plan, cut the prayer and Sabbath sentences from Team and The invitation (keep them in the Christian edition and Appendix E) and open "Who we are" with the methods committee and three secular modules. In the Christian edition, add: "The criteria are published. Any Christian module that meets them is funded; the board has committed to fund every committee-approved module or say publicly why not."

**8. The four things I always look for are missing, and the one comparable offered is the wrong one.** *New.*
No evaluation plan for the organization (who judges whether CEI met its targets); no sustainability beyond "Big Bettor" and "earned revenue later"; no letters of support (Harvard, Notre Dame, AllFaith and Markkula are all "in discussion"); no comparable organizations' budgets. *Budget v3, Aggressive:* "the v2 budget's CAIS-pace upside." *Financial plan:* "Conservative: revenue $100K, $600K, $1.3M," a sixfold rise called conservative; five-year totals of $7.7M and $10.9M repeat in the board brief.
*Why:* a two-founder volunteer group pacing itself against a well-funded safety institute invites the question it least wants, and the totals will be read as the real expectation.
*Fix:* rename "Conservative" to "Base," add a "Floor" path (flat $100K–$250K through 2028), and show three peers' year-one-to-three revenue from public filings (to verify on Candid or ProPublica). Name who evaluates CEI at months 12 and 18; list the letters you will secure. **The one page I want instead of this set:** a project sheet, "A tradition-reviewed test of whether AI acts on stated values": lead and hours; co-lead; two modules and the say–do pilot with dates; the three targets above; a budget with committed, pending and requested columns; what this funder's money buys; letters attached; three risks. Everything else is an appendix.

## 3. Should fix

1. **Independence caps collide with the budget.** *Funding model:* "No more than 15% of annual revenue from any single AI company ... in-kind support counts at fair value." On the $100K path that is $15K, while the plan offsets a $36K compute line "with researcher-access credits." Say which: credits from at least three vendors, or a disclosed 2027 exemption. Add a concentration rule for non-AI money ("any gift over 30% of annual revenue is reviewed by independent directors and disclosed"). *Carried over in part.*
2. **One lab is both the demand evidence and the evaluated party.** The Olah quote opens the executive summary, the Anthropic summit leads "Proof so far," "working contacts on Chris Olah's team" sit in the partners table, and the Christian edition repeats all three. Keep one mention, paired with the publish-unfavorable-results rule. *Carried over, partly addressed.*
3. **Two clocks.** *Executive summary:* "within 12 months of first-tranche funding"; *Financial plan:* "the 12-month clock starts at the first hire." Pick one; a funder holds you to the earlier.
4. **The headline proof point's validation is unfunded.** "Human validation of the coding planned for 2027," yet AI Governance "adds no spending in Tranche 1" and no budget line exists. Add a line or an owner.
5. **Legal structure still "to confirm."** A grant agreement will be signed by Social Good Fund; say so, and who owns data under the executed model. *Carried over.*
6. **The website lists a former ED.** It is the first thing I check. *Carried over.*
7. **Christian edition, "Give":** "$44K funds the computing and infrastructure ... $60K funds the Executive Director's first year." Church donors fund people. Replace with "$2,500 pays one theologian to review the encyclical module; $25K seats the first Protestant module's scholars; $60K founds the institution for a year," add a founding-circle goal and date, and grow the grieving-person line into a paragraph.
8. **Christian edition overclaims the report.** "How today's models represent Christian teaching": v1 has one encyclical. Write "how today's models represent *Magnifica humanitas*."
9. **Route the fellow through DELTA, and Templeton through a co-applicant.** DELTA's Faith and AI Faculty Fellowships pay $80K and admit independent scholars; the 2027–28 round closed Oct 26, 2026 ([page](https://ethics.nd.edu/fellowships-and-grants/faith-and-ai-faculty-fellowships/)). The "research fellow hosted with a university partner" is this fellowship. Build the Templeton 2027 OFI with a Notre Dame or Harvard co-PI, sized under the small-grant line (to verify).

## 5. What is strong

- Page-one candor: "all volunteer, about $17K raised," "no 2027 funding committed," "labs ask for dialogue, not tests." Keep every word.
- Independence as numbers, an embargo without edit rights, and a stated willingness to publish a poor result for a lab you work with.
- The two charters. No organization this size sends a conflict rule like "If the committee blocks a module, the board may not release it." Do not soften it.
- The dated tiers and "no role is hired until nine months of its cost is in hand." Fix the date, keep the rule.
- "Matching the ask to the funder" is the right instinct; item 4's table is what it needs behind it.
