# Board-chair review: CEI Business Plan 2027–2029

Read against the Oct 26 plan, the open-questions file, the earlier working notes and the evidence report. Figures are from the plan unless marked [verify].

## 1. Verdict

**Advise only for now; willing to chair from Q2 2027 if four conditions are met.** The research design is more mature than the organization meant to deliver it: no committed 2027 money, two executive directors gone in about a year, a three-person insider board and, most seriously, no settled answer to which legal entity a director joins or who owns the datasets. I cannot recruit directors into a structure I cannot describe. Conditions: (1) sponsorship agreement, IP ownership and D&O insurance in writing; (2) a floor budget with dated triggers and a named interim leader with hours; (3) every traction claim verified or cut, website corrected; (4) "Christian-led" defined in one sentence that still lets me fill the AI-research and law/finance seats.

## 2. Top problems, ranked

**1. Which entity does the board govern?** [JIMMY]
*Governance:* "fiscally sponsored by Social Good Fund ... sponsorship model and dataset ownership to confirm." The working notes show Model A chosen in June and Model C setup items in July; these are different organizations. Under Model A the sponsor's board controls the project, employs the ED, holds the grants with variance power and owns datasets created during sponsorship unless the agreement says otherwise; the California corporation's board governs a shell. **Fix:** before approaching any outside director, obtain the signed agreement and add a 150-word "Legal structure" box: entity, who controls funds, who employs staff, who owns data and code, fee rate, exit terms. Add a resolution releasing data and code under open licenses by default. State where the $17K is banked.

**2. The funding risk is listed, not planned for.** [JIMMY] + [TEXT]
*Financial plan:* "If only Tranche 1 is raised, we still publish the method and a smaller v1." Nothing covers $60K or $90K. "No committed 2027 funding" is row 21 of 24 in the risk table, with a circular mitigation: hire people with money you do not have. Foundations pay six to twelve months after first contact, and a sponsor releases salary only against money received [verify], so any first-half hire rests on "$50K from board members and major donors," the people this plan is recruiting. **Fix:** three dated tiers: "By March 31, 2027: under $60K received, stay all-volunteer, spend only on legal, sponsor, insurance and compute credits, v1 becomes a two-module preprint; $60–120K, contract the engineer first, president serves as interim ED; $173K+, as planned. No role is hired until nine months of its cost is in the sponsor account." Move funding to row 1 and date v1 from the first hire, not from this document.

**3. Nobody runs CEI until an ED exists.** [JIMMY]
*Team:* "Benchmark v1 is led by [name], [N] hours a week." That placeholder is the plan's most honest line. The president is a chief scientific officer at a company; the research director's location and availability are unstated; no interim ED or founder hours appear; employer consent for an outside officer role is unmentioned [verify]. **Fix:** "Until an ED starts, [founder] is unpaid interim ED at N hours/week, reported monthly to the board; v1 is led by [name] at N hours/week, accountable for the release date." If the founders cannot write the hours down, remove the 12-month date.

**4. The ED role is designed to fail a third time.** [JIMMY]
*What we are adding:* "starting fractional (50–60% time) and moving to full time as revenue allows." A 60% ED at $60K must raise $300K including their own pay, manage 40 volunteers, fix the sponsor, hire the engineer, build a board and ship v1: a full-time job at half pay, and the job two people have left. The reporting line is unclear (board, founder-president, or sponsor as legal employer). **Fix:** pick one honest design: (a) no ED in 2027, a part-time operations manager ($35–45K) plus the president as interim ED, reviewed in Q3; or (b) a full-time founding operator at $95–110K, funded before posting. Either way: "The ED reports to the board through the chair; the president does not supervise the ED." Do not post until money is received.

**5. Board arithmetic and hygiene.** [TEXT] + [JIMMY]
*Governance table:* "independent majority before second-tranche funds are spent." So $173K is spent under two founders plus a director who co-founded the president's earlier nonprofit: zero independents by most funders' test. Adding "3–4" reaches a majority only if four join. No bylaws are cited; no secretary or treasurer is named (California requires both [verify]); "insurance: to confirm," and California's volunteer-director shield depends on D&O cover [verify]. **Fix:** "At least four independents of seven before any salaried hire. D&O bound and bylaws with indemnification adopted before any independent joins. Treasurer and secretary named at the first meeting; a finance committee of the treasurer plus one independent; founders leave for an executive session every meeting." Replace the "named devil's advocate" with that session.

**6. The budget is tidy but incomplete.** [JIMMY] + [TEXT]
*2027 budget:* no founder-compensation line, though the finance notes leave founder pay undecided; no payroll taxes or benefits (about 10% for an employee; a contractor ED invites misclassification questions [verify]); no contingency; no reserve despite the promised three months (about $42K at Tranche 1 burn); no recruiting, website, travel or IRB fees. The "half-time" engineer at $50K buys roughly 500 hours at Bay Area contract rates, about ten hours a week [verify]; the compute line's own range runs $10K–$90K. **Fix:** write "Founder compensation 2027: $0" if that is the decision; add 10% contingency; restate the engineer as hours at a rate; add a probability-weighted revenue table.

**7. Forty volunteers at the labs being scored.** [TEXT] + [JIMMY]
*What we have built:* "day jobs include Google DeepMind, Meta, Scale AI, ByteDance." Principle 4 says evaluators must be independent of those they evaluate; the volunteer base is not. Their employment agreements typically claim AI-related work, so retroactive IP assignments will lose contributors, and what they cannot license cannot be released "open by default." **Fix:** a contributor license (CC BY 4.0 data, Apache 2.0 code) rather than assignment, with a representation that the employer does not claim the work; a published recusal rule ("no one employed by a scored lab authors or rates items for that lab's results"); embargoed results kept from volunteers at other labs; a plan for those who decline. Treat EthicsMosaic's 7,041 third-party codes as possibly unreleasable.

**8. "Christian-led" is undefined and will shape recruiting.** [JIMMY]
*Governance:* "The governing board will be Christian-led." The chair, a majority, or every seat? The AI-industry, academic and law/finance seats draw from pools that shrink under an all-Christian rule; the earlier notes' slate is six men from one philanthropic network [verify]. Secular funders will ask, and "a Christian-founded CEI is trusted beyond Christian communities" is tested by the board before any benchmark. **Fix:** decide, then one sentence: "The chair and a majority of directors are practicing Christians; no individual seat carries a faith requirement; the methods and advisory committees have none." If every seat is intended, say so and accept the narrower pool. Put it in the bylaws or nowhere.

**9. Independence-policy loopholes.** [TEXT]
*Funding model:* "in-kind compute from several vendors is preferred to cash from one lab." Compute from a scored lab is the conflict, not the mitigation. "Underwrite the Executive Director, $60K" invites one donor to own the leader. The $25K review catches directors' own gifts with no recusal rule, while a $20K lab gift passes untouched. **Fix:** "In-kind support counts at fair value toward the 15% cap. Any gift from an AI company, its founders or employees is disclosed and reviewed at any size. Directors recuse from review of their own gifts. No donor is named sponsor of a staff role."

**10. The methods committee has no charter and no members.** [TEXT]
*Governance:* "Decisions about which traditions are included and how they are scored rest with the independent methods committee, not the board." Ceding core program decisions to a body that does not exist, needs "at least two secular philosophers" and has no budget line is acceptable only with a charter. **Fix:** one page at the first meeting: the board appoints and removes members on the president's nomination; two-year terms; public membership; decisions final unless the board finds a legal or financial bar; conflicts disclosed; first five members named by a date; a small honorarium line in Tranche 1.

**11. Claims a candidate checks in ten minutes.** [JIMMY] + [TEXT]
*Risks:* "Our website lists a former ED" is rated Medium; it is the cheapest fix and the most damaging. The evidence report found no CEI names in the 5ICEAI program, no public Governance Symphony paper and no verifiable Harvard or Notre Dame tie, yet the plan names a Harvard program, Notre Dame scholars and a lab contact while the checklist admits consent is unconfirmed. "Reported by the Washington Post and New York Times" reads as if they reported the president's participation [verify]. A founding board inherits any overstatement. **Fix:** every traction claim gets a link or is cut; "accepted" only where a program lists it; replace "a direct line to Chris Olah's team" with "working contacts at one frontier lab" (a line into a lab you will score undercuts independence); website fixed within seven days.

**12. The internal checklist is in the document, and the document is too long.** [TEXT]
*Internal checklist (remove before sending):* it tells a reader the numbers are unconfirmed, a transition's wording is unsettled and names were used before consent. One mis-send ends a board conversation. At 11,000 words with no contents, a 24-row risk table and 500 words of theology mid-document, nobody finishes it on a phone. **Fix:** move the checklist to a separate file today. Add a two-page board-candidate brief up front (entity, money in hand, obligations, insurance, hours); move measurement design, landscape and "Our foundation" to appendices; send the Christian edition to Christian audiences instead of embedding it.

## 3. Keep

- Page one admits "all volunteer, about $17K raised" and "no 2027 funding committed." Most founders bury this.
- Independence rules are numbers: 15% per company, 40% pooled, fixed embargo without edits, no badges or leaderboards, negative results published.
- "What we will not do in 2027," one flagship, and a quarterly continue/change/stop review per program.
- The risk table names real weaknesses: crowded field, lagging public record, secular credibility, geopolitical perception.
- The measurement design (capability correlation, rater agreement, two judge vendors, canary strings, held-out splits) is better than the organizational plan and is the reason to keep talking.

## 4. First board meeting: five decisions in 90 days

1. **Structure.** Adopt the legal-structure statement; confirm the sponsorship model and IP terms in the signed agreement; adopt the open-license default; bind D&O; adopt bylaws with indemnification; name a secretary and treasurer; adopt the conflict-of-interest policy.
2. **Money.** Adopt the three-tier floor budget with the March 31 trigger and the nine-months-in-the-account rule; decide engineer-first versus ED-first; set 2027 founder compensation explicitly; set a give-or-get range for directors.
3. **Leadership.** Minute the interim ED and v1 lead with hours per week; approve the ED design; fix the reporting line through the chair; appoint a two-independent search committee.
4. **Independence.** Adopt the methods-committee charter and name first members by date; adopt the volunteer recusal rule and contributor license; close the gift-policy loopholes.
5. **Composition and candor.** Define "Christian-led"; set four independents of seven before any hire; fill the law/finance seat first; set Q1 deliverables: claims audit, website fixed, volunteer agreements signed, checklist removed.
