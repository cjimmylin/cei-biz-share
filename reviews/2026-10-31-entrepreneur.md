# Review: serial founder of research organizations

*Read Oct 31, 2026: the general plan with appendices A–E, the board brief, the technical brief, the Christian edition, both charters and Budget v3 (all five sheets), against the Oct 27 reviews, INTERNAL-CHECKLIST.md and OPEN-QUESTIONS.md. ## 1. Verdict

I would back the two founders and the behavioral-measurement idea with a small check tied to a 90-day result, and I would not back the $308K institutional plan as written. It funds an institution (an Executive Director, a board of seven or eight, a methods committee, an advisory committee, two charters, a Scholar Group) before it has a product, a user or a stated number of founder hours, and the one thing that decides whether CEI matters, whether anyone outside CEI will run its measurement, is untested and absent from the 90-day priorities. The design is good and the documents are unusually honest; the sequencing is backwards. Your own workbook already holds the right 2027: the conservative path's milestone row ("a two-module preprint (one secular, one faith) with the say-do pilot ... No Executive Director") is the plan I would fund. The aggressive path is the upside, not the plan.

**The next 30 days, if I were the founder:**

1. Run the no-reference-answer pilot with volunteers only (sycophancy probe, reasoning-stability task, say–do consistency) on three open-weight models and publish a two-page result with transcripts. No board chair, no ED posting, no charter adoption until it exists.
2. Make three calls and nothing else: the CEFE-AI (AllFaith) consortium, one evaluation organization or safety-institute team for a design-partner pilot, and one Catholic center for the encyclical co-lead, each with a written offer drawn from the technical brief's "How to work with us" table.
3. Write both founders' weekly hours into the plan, count the people it recruits in 2027 (about 35; see Must fix 4), and cut the 12-month list to what those hours plus 500 engineer hours can deliver. Re-date v1 from the first hire and print the expected date.

## 2. Must fix before this goes to a funder or board candidate

**1. There is no "one thing"; there are seven co-equal 12-month outcomes.** (Carried over: faith funder 5; checklist 10, undecided.)
*Where:* Measuring impact, seven rows dated "12 months"; Strategy, "for the next 12 months one leads." *Problem:* the "one flagship" is four benchmarks, a say–do module, a fifth outside-authored module, a local-run kit, a harness integration, per-benchmark briefs, two papers, a board, two committees, an ED and an engineer. A funder who sees focus claimed and then this list discounts the timeline; a founder with a day job cannot carry it. *Fix:* open Measuring impact with: "The one outcome of the first 12 months: a pre-registered result on two modules (one secular, one faith) plus the say–do and sycophancy tasks, which at least one named outside team has run. Everything else is a means to that or a stretch." Then cut: the moral-psychology module (exploratory, no profile claims), the fifth module, the advisory committee, the Scholar Group outreach and convening, the per-benchmark briefs, and the ED posting until the pilot result exists. Governance Symphony stays volunteer and off the president's plate.

**2. The months 1–3 pilot is forbidden by CEI's own charters and proves the wrong thing.**
*Where:* Board brief, "Months 1–3: ... run a development-split pilot (three open models, 50 items per module) and publish its numbers"; Tradition Partner Charter §2, "CEI staff do not write or edit a tradition's reference answers ... and never for a tradition that has no partner"; Technical brief, v1 scope, "no model is scored until the methods committee signs off on the validation results." *Problem:* no module has a partner panel today (no Catholic theologians; scholarly leads for all four benchmarks are open) and the committee does not exist, so the pilot can have neither reference answers nor sign-off. It is the only proof CEI can produce without money, and the charters adopted to satisfy reviewers block it. *Fix:* build the pilot on the three tasks that need no tradition reference answers: reasoning quality (consistency across variants), the sycophancy probe (paired, no-cue baseline, false-attribution arm) and say–do consistency (action versus the model's own stated judgment). Add to both charters: "Work labeled pilot or exploratory on the development split may be published before a partner panel or the committee is seated, carrying that label." The field's lesson: AllFaith is 150 questions on 14 models and reached national press because it had a finding (religious viewpoints largely absent), not a method ([Deseret News, May 2026](https://www.deseret.com/faith/2026/05/26/studies-find-religious-bias-in-ai-models/)). Your 90-day product is a finding.

**3. Nobody outside CEI has asked for this, and the 90-day plan does not try to find them.**
*Where:* Partners, "Partners we still need: An AI evaluation organization (harness integration and a pre-release pilot)"; Key assumptions, "Demand ... A pilot with one evaluation organization before release"; the five 90-day priorities (none names a user); Appendix A, "Who we serve. Researchers, developers and auditors ...; policymakers ...; and, above all, the patients, students, workers ..." *Problem:* across six documents no sentence records anyone outside CEI asking to run, cite or fund the benchmark; the Olah quote asks for critics, the Barna figure measures pastors' preferences, and the plan's own evidence says "labs ask for dialogue, not tests." Benchmarks are adopted by a first user who co-designed them; this plan builds for a year before meeting one. *Fix:* replace priority 3 with: "Sign one design partner: three structured interviews with named evaluation teams, and one written agreement to run the development split and give feedback in exchange for early access. Publish what they said they would run." Replace "Who we serve" with: "Our first users are evaluation organizations and open-model labs, because frontier labs have asked for dialogue, not tests. Our beneficiaries are the people who live with AI decisions they did not shape."

**4. The plan never states founder hours, and it recruits about 35 people in a year.** (Carried over: board chair 3, faith funder 3, checklist 1.)
*Where:* Team, "the president serves as unpaid interim leader ... (proposed; hours to confirm)"; "Benchmark v1 is led by [name], [N] hours a week," still a placeholder in a document "for prospective board members and funders." *Problem:* the 2027 recruits the documents require: a chair and a cohort of 3–4; a methods committee of 5–7; an advisory committee; an ED, engineer, co-lead, measurement advisor, secular ML co-author and fellow; at least three panel scholars per module for four modules (twelve, of whom zero Catholics today); three lab or evaluation researchers; one evaluation organization. Roughly 35–40 founder-led recruiting conversations, on top of raising $300K from eight named funders and leading v1, while the president is a biotech chief scientific officer. A reader on the funding side will do this count. *Fix:* a "Hours and load" paragraph: "Jimmy Lin gives N hours a week, M of them to v1; Rui Guo gives N hours a week. In 2027 we will recruit about [N] people; the plan is sized to those hours." Under about 30 combined hours a week, halve the list as in Must fix 1.

**5. The aggressive path's 2027 cannot happen on the plan's own facts, and neither path is a floor.**
*Where:* Financial plan, "On the aggressive path, v1 ships in 2027"; the workbook's Aggressive 2027 milestone row; against "no 2027 funding committed yet," "foundations pay six to twelve months after first contact," and "No role is hired until nine months of its cost is in hand." *Problem:* nine months of ED, engineer and co-lead is about $92K in the sponsor account before the first hire; foundation conversations open in November; so the first hire is Q3 2027 at best and v1 is Q3 2028. The "conservative" path grows six-fold in 2028 ($100K to $600K); no column shows the modal outcome for a volunteer faith-and-tech nonprofit, $100K–$150K a year for several years. The aggressive 2028 ($800K) buys the same four people, now full time, plus $98K of reserve; later years follow a "CAIS-pace upside" [verify what funded that pace; the plan's own funding section says the nearest equivalent funder has no open AI calls]. *Fix:* (a) relabel the aggressive 2027 column "if Tranche 1 is in hand by March 31, 2027" and add to the plan: "On the current pipeline we expect the first hire in [quarter] and v1 in [quarter] 2028." (b) Add a Floor path to the workbook: $120K a year flat, what it maintains (two modules, six open-weight models, one run a year, contract engineer only, no staff), so the board knows what survival looks like.

**6. Three bodies and two charters before v1, an unbudgeted honoraria line, and a quarterly volunteer committee on the critical path.**
*Where:* Methods Committee Charter §1(d), final authority over "whether a module may be scored and released"; §5, "Meets at least quarterly and before every scoring freeze"; §8, "CEI provides staff support and a budget line for members' honoraria (amount for board decision)"; Governance step 4, "Form a multi-tradition advisory committee"; Budget v3, which carries no honoraria line on either path and no staff to give support. *Problem:* the charters' content is right, but as adopted they put a body that does not exist, meeting quarterly, between a volunteer organization and every public result, and commit money the workbook does not hold. Funders of pre-product organizations read governance weight as a substitute for work. *Fix:* keep both charters and add to each: "This charter takes force at the first scoring freeze for a public release. Until then a three-person interim methods panel advises, and pilot results carry the exploratory label." Defer the advisory committee ("formed with the first outside-authored module"). Add the honoraria line to the workbook or write "$0 in 2027; members serve pro bono" in the charter.

**7. Distribution has no owner, no cadence and an out-of-date mechanism.**
*Where:* Strategy, "one-command runs in standard evaluation harnesses such as Inspect Evals"; workbook, "Full evaluation runs per year ... 1, 2, 2, 2, 3" against the 2031 milestone "the suite is a standing reference for evaluators and labs." *Problem:* Inspect Evals no longer accepts code submissions for new evaluations; you register one you host yourself ([Inspect Evals contribution guide](https://ukgovernmentbeis.github.io/inspect_evals/contributing)) [verify the live process]. Two runs a year cannot be a reference when models ship monthly; the engineer line is about 500 hours for the harness, pipeline, server, kit and integration together; nobody is named to run the suite on release day and publish. *Fix:* name a distribution owner (the co-lead) and a cadence: "The development split runs on every major open-weight release within 14 days, automated and cheap; the private split runs on the formal schedule. Each run ships a short profile note." Replace the Inspect Evals sentence with "an Inspect task hosted in CEI's repository and listed in the Inspect Evals Register, with a published results schema."

**8. The three differentiators are each what someone else is better placed to build, and the closest peer has not been called.**
*Where:* Risks, "lead with what others lack (the say–do test, a scholar-reviewed Magnifica humanitas module, the governance corpus)"; Partners, "AllFaith benchmark consortium ... conversations not yet started"; Appendix B, Gloo "A natural collaborator." *Problem:* agentic say–do is what labs do best internally and the Jakkli et al. audit already ran a version; a scholar-authored encyclical module is what Notre Dame's $50.8M DELTA network can build with real theologians; the governance corpus is not a benchmark. Gloo is a public company that reports tuning its own models on its Christian-worldview benchmark data ([Gloo, June 2026](https://gloo.com/gloo-news/fai-insights-report-june-2026)) [verify]: a commercial scorer with an incentive, not a natural collaborator. AllFaith is open, small and backed by four universities, so CEI's method is a real upgrade for it. If a lab ships a values benchmark first, that helps CEI (it validates the category and CEI becomes the independent re-run); if AllFaith or DELTA ships a scholar-authored faith module first, CEI loses the module and its faith funders. *Fix:* replace the competitive paragraph with: "Our durable asset is the open method plus independence, not any one module. If a lab publishes a values benchmark, we re-run it independently and compare. If a university consortium publishes a scholar-authored faith module first, we offer our behavioral tasks and method as its measurement layer rather than compete; we will open that conversation with CEFE-AI by [date]." Relabel Gloo "a commercial benchmark publisher with its own models; we compare with it and do not expect to collaborate."

## 3. Should fix

1. **Hiring order contradicts itself.** Financial plan: "The first priority is an Executive Director, a half-time research engineer, a technical co-lead"; Shared assumptions: "Hiring order: engineer and technical co-lead first, then the research fellow" (no ED); OPEN-QUESTIONS Oct 28: "engineer and research fellow first." Write: "Order: engineer, then co-lead, then the ED decision after the pilot, then the fellow."
2. **Carried over, text right, fact missing:** sponsorship model, data and code ownership, D&O and bylaws (board chair 1; checklist 6); the website's former ED (board chair 11); a probability-weighted pipeline (board chair 6; "Committed vs. pipeline" still lists names without dates or odds). Give each a done-by date in the board brief's "Still open" list.
3. **Moral psychology is still a full v1 module** in Appendix A, though the technical brief says "v1 makes no developmental-stage claims." Label it exploratory in both (checklist 10).
4. **Two 18-month clocks.** Strategy: "within 18 months of first-tranche funding, the board will review" and "If no one outside CEI has run the suite within 18 months of release, the board will not fund a second version." Release is a year after funding, so the hard rule fires at month 30. Pick one.
5. **The technical brief's 90-day pilot item contradicts itself:** "three open-weight models, 50 items per module ... capability correlation on 20 or more open models." Two items, two dates.
6. **Partner veto over judges conflicts with the fixed judge panel.** Tradition Partner Charter §4 gives a veto over "the choice of AI judges for that module"; the technical brief says "One fixed judge panel ... scores every model." Per-module judges confound the between-module correlations the committee must review. Write: "A partner may object to a judge for cause; the objection is recorded as dissent and the panel stays fixed."
7. **Panel size is undefined for frameworks.** "At least three scholars from the tradition, drawn from more than one school" is three or twelve philosophers for normative ethics. Say which, and add a succession line: with four panels and 60-day withdrawal, one will churn in a year.
8. **The Scholar Group is a recruiting channel in 2027, not a program.** "outreach begins Q1 2027" to 650 people is a distraction. Write: "In 2027 we contact the roughly 20 scholars the v1 panels need; broader outreach and the first convening wait for Tranche 2."
9. **The $1,200 per model per module figure carries no multiplier** for three framings, two formats, paraphrase sets and a two-vendor judge panel, while the brief promises "a dry-run cost per model." Put the pilot's dry-run number in the plan before the ask goes out.

## 5. What is strong

- The dated funding tiers and the nine-months-in-hand rule: real discipline, rare at this stage. Do not let the aggressive path erode them.
- The construct separation and the two behavioral tasks (say–do, sycophancy): the only parts a lab can act on and the only parts that can be piloted without a tradition panel. They are the product.
- The independence rules with numbers, the embargo without edit rights, and "we publish unfavorable results, including for labs we work with."
- The candor: "labs ask for dialogue, not tests," "no CEI paper is yet published or preprinted," "'many traditions' is our direction, not yet our state," and the board brief's "Still open, stated plainly."
- The workbook: formula-driven, two paths on one staffing plan, an assumptions sheet and a change log. Its conservative 2027 is the better plan.
