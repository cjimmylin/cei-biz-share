# Review: a nonprofit business-planning professor reads the CEI plan set

Reviewed Oct 31, 2026: the general plan with Appendices A–E, the board brief, the technical brief, the Christian edition, the two charters and Budget v3 (all five sheets), against the Oct 27 reviews, `plan/INTERNAL-CHECKLIST.md` and `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`. Resolved findings are not re-raised; open ones are marked **carried over**. G = general plan, T = technical brief, B = board brief, C = Christian edition, W = workbook.

## 1. Verdict

**Grade: B.** The research half is A-grade: the construct separation, the key-assumptions table, independence rules with numbers, two charters that make "no one controls the scoring" a mechanism, and a candor most founders cannot manage. The organizational half is C-grade, and it is the half a funder reads in 20 minutes. The rubric line that costs the plan most is **management team and execution capacity**: the plan promises benchmark v1 within 12 months, and the person accountable is still "[name], [N] hours a week"; both founders are part time with hours "to confirm"; the "half-time" engineer is 500 hours by the plan's own footnote; and the Executive Director the budget funds is a design the same paragraph says the board will choose against. The financials compound it: the plan cannot say what $202K buys, the revenue line has no sources, and Measuring impact measures outputs. Most of this is on the founder's own checklist, which is why the grade is B and not lower: it is a week of decisions from A-.

## 2. Must fix

**1. Nobody named runs the flagship (carried over).** G, Team and governance: "Benchmark v1 is led by [name], [N] hours a week, accountable to the board for the release date"; "the president serves as unpaid interim leader ... (proposed; hours to confirm)." *Why:* a funder stops at this sentence. *Fix:* "Until an Executive Director starts, Jimmy Lin is unpaid interim leader at N hours a week, reporting monthly to the board. Benchmark v1 is led by [name] at N hours a week; the technical co-lead is [name]. Rui Guo gives N hours a week to research direction." If names are not available, write "to be named by [date]; the 12-month clock does not start until they are."

**2. The Executive Director in the budget is neither design the board will choose between.** G, What we are adding, item 1: "starting fractional (about 60% time) ... Before posting, the board will choose between a part-time operations manager with the president as interim leader ... and a full-time founding operator funded before the role is posted." The budget table, executive summary, B and W all fund a third design, a 60% ED at $60K. *Why:* the most important hire is described three ways in one paragraph. *Fix:* either delete the "choose between" sentence and say why a fractional leader can do the job this time, or budget a range ("Operating leader: $45K–$105K depending on the design adopted at the first board meeting") and change W's ED pay path to match.

**3. Tiers, tranches and the 12-month clock do not reconcile.** G, Financial plan: "at about $200K or more we proceed as planned," two lines after "Tranche 2 ... funds the public release"; the executive summary says Tranche 2 "unlocks the public release of benchmark v1." The tiers skip $120K–$200K; the $60K–$120K tier names a hire but no deliverable; the clock starts "at the first hire" (Financial plan) and at "first-tranche funding" (executive summary, programs table, B, T); G, Strategy, runs two different 18-month clocks. *Why:* a funder asks "what does my $100K buy?" and gets four answers. *Fix:* one table, verbatim in G, B and W: under $60K by Mar 31, 2027: all-volunteer, two-module preprint with the say–do pilot; $60K–$120K: engineer contracted, two-module preprint, v1 in 2028 (W's conservative path; say so); $120K–$200K: engineer and co-lead, four benchmarks on the development split, no test split or profile reports; $200K+: full v1 within 12 months of the first paid hire, public release contingent on Tranche 2 by [month]. One clock: "months count from the first paid hire."

**4. The money does not match the work (carried over in part).** G, budget: "Expert annotation and tradition leads (stipends and gold-set rating) — $40K" for four benchmarks plus say–do. Appendix A needs three scholars to ratify 300–500 items per module plus judge validation of "about 100 items × 3 models × 2 raters per module": roughly 8,000–10,000 expert judgments, under $5 each, before any lead's honorarium. Tradition Partner Charter §4 promises "the 2027 plan budgets $20K–$40K per module," which is $100K–$200K for five; W prices later modules at $30K. The invitation table omits the technical co-lead ($12K), IT/travel ($10K) and the reserve ($10K), and lists a full-year fellow ($75K) against a half-year line ($38K). The engineer is "half-time" in every document and a quarter in the footnote. *Why:* the central deliverable is priced at a quarter of what the plan's own charter promises. *Fix:* price v1 at W's unit cost (5 × $30K = $150K, raising the ask to about $420K) or cut v1 to two modules plus the behavioral tasks, the fallback the checklist names, and say so in B and C. Write "part-time (about 500 hours)" or fund 1,000 hours. Add "Fund the technical co-lead — $12K" to the invitation.

**5. Measuring impact measures outputs, and its targets restate its metrics.** G, Measuring impact: "Target: Share of items endorsed, reported per module"; "Target: Krippendorff's α reported per tradition and dimension." Reporting a number is not a target. Every 12-month row is an output; influence is "tracked, not required"; the only true outcome sits at "10+ years." The section cites Bridgespan's intended-impact discipline and names no intended impact the organization will be held to. The assumptions table has the same gap: no row says what result would change course. *Why:* a board cannot hold leadership to "reported." *Fix:* thresholds plus one near-term outcome: "at least 80% of gold items per module endorsed by two of three tradition scholars; α ≥ 0.67 on every reported dimension; capability correlation below 0.7 on at least three of five tasks; by month 18, at least one organization outside CEI has run the suite and reported a result." Move the 18-month no-second-version rule from Strategy into this table as the stop rule, and add a "What would make us stop" column to the assumptions table.

**6. The five-year revenue line has no sources, and the realistic case is labeled "conservative."** G, Five-year outlook: the aggressive path "follows that budget's upside case"; W: "the v2 budget's CAIS-pace upside." No year is split by channel, no anchor funder is named, and the identified 2027 pipeline (a deferred $48K application plus "at least $50K from board members") is about $100K: W's conservative path. Contingency is 0% in 2027–28 "when every dollar is committed," the years it is most needed. *Why:* $308K to $5.0M in four years, justified by another organization's pace, is the first line a finance-literate director tests. *Fix:* a revenue-by-channel row per year, a named target per channel for 2027–28 with a probability, 10% contingency in 2027–28, and on page one: "The $308K ask is the aggressive path; our identified pipeline today supports the conservative one."

**7. The executive summary does not stand alone.** It names no founder ("CEI was founded by Christians who believe..."), gives "about $300K" whose tranches sum to $308K on the next line, never says what success is (B's "our success is rigorous, open, published work" is absent), omits "nothing is committed for 2027," and carries an 80-word Governance Symphony bullet. *Why:* most funders read only this page. *Fix:* open with B's three-sentence thesis; add one line naming both founders with degrees, titles and "both part time"; write "about $308K"; add "Nothing is committed for 2027; about $17K has been raised to date"; cut the Governance Symphony bullet to its finding and its count.

**8. Drafting notes and consent-contingent names sit in the circulated text.** G, Partners table names a Harvard Law School program and adds "(to be named only with its agreement)"; the same row names a Notre Dame institute the checklist says is unconfirmed. G, Financial plan: "about $17K received to date (... to be confirmed)"; Team: "(proposed; hours to confirm)." *Why:* naming an institution and confessing that consent is pending does the harm in one clause. *Fix:* until consent is written, "in discussion with a program at Harvard Law School and an ethics institute at Notre Dame"; strike every "to confirm" from the body.

## 3. Should fix

1. **Fifth module and local-run kit: "stretch" in one place, deliverables in five.** G, Measuring impact: "Stretch, if Tranche 2 closes by mid-2027: a fifth module ... and a local-run kit"; Appendix A, T Summary and Deliverables, B "What CEI builds" and C list both as v1. The stretch label is the honest one; use it everywhere.
2. **The 90-day plan breaks the hiring rule.** G, priorities: "Post the Executive Director role and begin interviews" by January 2027, against "no role is posted until nine months of its cost is in hand" and "Before posting, the board will choose," with the cohort seated in Q1 2027 and $0 committed. Replace with "Draft the operating-leader role for the board's first meeting."
3. **Contractor status vs. sponsor as employer.** W, Assumptions: "0% in 2027 while everyone is a contractor"; G, Legal structure: the sponsor "is the legal employer of any staff." A 60% ED reporting to the board is unlikely to pass California's ABC test as a contractor (to verify with the sponsor and counsel); exposure about $30K. Budget it or state the sponsor's position.
4. **"Our largest risks are focus and founder capacity, not demand"** (G, Risks) holds only because success is defined as publishing; Appendix C says labs want dialogue, not tests. Add demand as a mission-level risk and put the 18-month rule in that row.
5. **The encyclical module and the charter (carried over).** Tradition Partner Charter §2: CEI staff never write a tradition's reference answers "for a tradition that has no partner." The *Magnifica humanitas* prototype is "in active development" with no Catholic scholar; the checklist says present it as proposed. Write: "A CEI draft exists and will be rewritten under a Catholic panel; no item is final until that panel is named."
6. **Two method contradictions in T.** v1 scope: "no model is scored until the methods committee signs off on the validation results," yet those results need 20+ open models scored on the development split; write "no profile report is published and no test-split run begins until..." Data protocol: items "with unresolved ambiguity are dropped, not averaged" vs. Validation, both charters and Methods Committee Charter §6.4: contested items are "kept and labeled." Keep and label.
7. **Length and structure.** Body 9,450 words, appendices 3,900, 49 letter pages; a 20-minute reader finishes the summary and one section. The appendix move works: "How we measure, in brief" is a good stand-in and the body reads without Appendix A. But "Our solution and theory of change" carries four tables (move partners and programs to "What we have built"); the four "success is publishing" passages and the "Our niche"/Landscape duplicate remain (carried over); and "Who we serve" and "The limits of measurement" are mission text parked in Appendix A.
8. **Two unreadable paragraphs.** G, the governance paragraph beginning "The governing board will be Christian-led" (170 words, nine clauses) and "Legal structure" (one 120-word sentence). Make each a list.
9. **Three-year targets exceed the budget.** G, Measuring impact: "at least five tradition modules beyond v1" by three years; W aggressive has eight modules in 2029 (three beyond v1), conservative six (one). "Seven core partners by 2029" (Strategy) has no line. Match the targets to W or raise W.
10. **Christian edition promise.** C, What success looks like: "how today's models represent Christian teaching" means one encyclical; write "represent the encyclical's teaching." Keep C's "Give" ladder matched to the invitation when must-fix 4 changes the lines.

## 5. What is strong

- **The key-assumptions table and the 18-month no-second-version rule.** Nine assumptions, each with a test, and one real stop rule: the most falsifiable theory of change I have seen in a startup nonprofit plan.
- **Candor as structure, not tone.** "No CEI paper is yet published or preprinted"; "'many traditions' is our direction, not yet our state"; B's "Still open, stated plainly." Add to this list, never trim it.
- **Independence with numbers and two charters.** 15%/40%/$25K, embargo without edit rights, a tradition's veto over its own module, a methods-committee block the board cannot override: a value turned into a mechanism.
- **The appendix move works.** "How we measure, in brief" lets the body stand alone; Appendices A–C are reference-grade and should stay out of the body.
- **The board brief.** Two pages, the best thesis statement in the set, honest risks, a clear ask. It should be the model for the executive summary, not the reverse.

2. Which operating-leader design is the plan: the 60% ED at $60K the budget funds, or one of the two the board is told to choose between?
3. What does $202K buy, full v1 or "a smaller v1," and what is the deliverable at $120K–$200K?
4. Is any 2028 revenue attached to a named prospective funder, and what is the basis for $800K beyond the v2 budget's "CAIS-pace" line?
5. Will the 2027 ED and engineer be contractors or Social Good Fund employees, and has the sponsor confirmed its position?
6. Is the *Magnifica humanitas* prototype a draft to be rewritten by a Catholic panel, or an item bank to be reviewed?
7. What endorsement share, agreement floor and capability correlation would make v1 a "high standard" release, and what result would make the board stop?
8. Where do v1's raters and panels come from in months 4–8 if Scholar Group outreach begins in Q1 2027 and no one has yet been contacted?
