# Nonprofit-management review: can the people and money described execute this plan?

*Stance: an executive director and chief operating officer who has run fiscally sponsored projects and grown research nonprofits from $100K to $5M a year. Read in full, Oct 31, 2026: the general plan with appendices A–E, the board brief, the technical brief, the Christian edition, both charters and Budget v3 (all five sheets); the Oct 27 reviews, the internal checklist and the founder's Oct 27–28 answers as context. "Carried over" marks an earlier finding still unresolved; "to verify" marks a fact not in the documents.*

## 1. Verdict

I would not take the Executive Director job as written, and I would not yet recommend the institutional ask to a funder; I would recommend a scoped research grant once a technical lead is named. The measurement design, the charters and the workbook are better than most I see at this stage. What decides it is one question every other problem flows from: **who runs CEI in 2027, for how many hours, on what money.** The plan says the ED design is still a board choice, yet every budget line, ask table and prayer request already sells a 60%-time, $60K contractor ED whose first task is to raise the money that pays for the role, hired by a board that does not yet exist, in an organization that has lost two EDs in about a year. Until the founders write down their own hours and pick one leadership design, the 12-month clock has no one holding it.

## 2. Must fix before this goes to a funder or board candidate

**M1. The ED design is undecided in the text and decided everywhere else.** (Carried over: board chair #4.)
*Plan, "What we are adding in 2027" §1:* "starting fractional (about 60% time) … Before posting, the board will choose between a part-time operations manager with the president as interim leader (reviewed in Q3 2027) and a full-time founding operator funded before the role is posted; … no role is posted until nine months of its cost is in hand." Against that: *"Five priorities for the next 90 days" §2:* "Post the Executive Director role and begin interviews" (with $17K in hand); *budget table, invitation table and board brief:* "Executive Director (fractional, about 60% time) $60K"; *Christian edition, "Give":* "$60K funds the Executive Director's first year."
*Why:* a candidate reads a 60%-time, no-benefits, nine-month contract reporting to a board not yet seated, at a pro-rated $100K below the Bay Area small-org band in CEI's own salary brief ($105K–$145K; the "$50K–$70K at 50–60% time" is a pro-rating in that brief, not a benchmark). Who takes it: a semi-retired executive, a junior operations hire, or a multi-client fractional operator; each fails differently, and the plan should say which it wants. A funder reads the contradiction as a decision not yet made.
*Fix:* decide now; I recommend the first option. Replace §1 with: "In 2027 the president serves as unpaid interim leader at [N] hours a week, reporting monthly to the board through the chair. The first operations hire is a part-time operations and grants manager (about $40K, employed by the sponsor), posted only when nine months of its loaded cost has been received. In Q3 2027 the board decides whether to recruit a full-time Executive Director for 2028, funded before posting." Change the $60K line, both ask tables and the Christian edition to match; delete "Post the Executive Director role" from the 90-day list; pro-rate the line, since the earliest start is Q2–Q3 2027.

**M2. The pipeline from $17K to $308K has no amounts, dates, probabilities or owners, and the calendar makes 2027 the conservative year.** (Carried over: board chair #2 and #6.)
*Plan, "Committed vs. pipeline":* "Committed: about $17K. Pending: none. Targeted for 2027: Foresight Institute (about $48K, application drafted), Faith Work & Tech Round 2, Templeton, Lilly, Humanity AI members, Future of Life Institute and Survival and Flourishing Fund, and at least $50K from board members and major donors."
*Why:* the plan's own risk row says foundations pay six to twelve months after first contact. Foresight was not applied for in the Oct 31 round (OPEN-QUESTIONS §3.1). Templeton runs from a summer inquiry to a funding decision the following summer ([Templeton](https://www.templeton.org/grants/apply-for-grant); [CASRAI](https://www.casrai.org/guides/john-templeton-foundation-research-grants)), so it cannot fund 2027. What can arrive by March 31, 2027 is board and major-donor gifts plus a rapid-response grant: the under-$60K or $60K–$120K tier. The plan still presents the aggressive path as "the plan," and names no one who writes the proposals.
*Fix:* add a pipeline table (funder, ask, stage, earliest decision, probability, owner, expected value) and state the probability-weighted 2027 total beside the $308K. Reorder: "Our base case for 2027 is the conservative path ($100K: engineer first, president as interim leader). The $308K plan is what we execute if the aggressive raise closes." Name who writes each proposal; if a contractor, budget $8K–$12K (grant writing is a professional-services exemption under California's contractor law; to verify with counsel).

**M3. The nine-months rule is undefined and has a contractor-shaped hole.**
*Plan, Financial plan:* "No role is hired until nine months of its cost is in hand, and the 12-month clock starts at the first hire." *"What we are adding" §2:* the engineer is "contracted first to avoid hiring delay." *Governance table step 2:* "independent majority before any salaried hire." *Board brief, months 1–3:* "contract the research engineer; seat the board chair and founding cohort." *Board and technical briefs:* "within 12 months of first-tranche funding."
*Why:* "in hand" could mean pledged or received; "hired" and "salaried" exclude contractors, so the first paid person can start before the board or the money; the cost is unloaded (M4); the clock is anchored to first funding in two documents and first hire in one; and the rule tells candidates a truth, a nine-month job, without saying it.
*Fix:* "No paid role, employee or contractor, starts until nine months of its fully loaded cost has been received into the sponsor's account for CEI. Every 2027 role is an initial nine-to-twelve-month term, renewable on funding, and candidates are told so. The 12-month clock to v1 starts the day the first paid role starts." Use it in all three documents.

**M4. "Everyone is a contractor in 2027" is a misclassification plan and understates personnel by about $13K.** (Carried over: board chair #6; v3 chose the opposite.)
*Workbook, Assumptions H6:* "0% in 2027 while everyone is a contractor; 25% from the first employee in 2028." *Plan footnote:* the same.
*Why:* under California's ABC test (AB 5) a worker is an employee unless the hiring entity proves all three prongs, including that the work is outside its usual course of business ([Nonprofit Law Blog](https://nonprofitlawblog.com/california-ab-5-employee-or-independent-contractor/); [LCW](https://www.lcwlegal.com/news/top-5-for-ab-5-legal-considerations-for-nonprofits-working-with-independent-contractors/)). An ED cannot pass that prong; an engineer building the core product is doubtful unless engaged through a bona fide firm. Under a comprehensive sponsorship the sponsor is the employer and runs payroll on its terms (to verify in the agreement).
*Fix:* load the ED and engineer lines at about 12% in 2027 (employer payroll taxes plus workers' compensation), about $13K, or restate the engineer as a firm engagement; carry the loaded figure into the nine-months rule. Replace H6 with: "12% in 2027 for sponsor-employed staff; 25% from 2028 when benefits begin."

**M5. The sponsorship fee matches neither model, and the model is still unwritten.** (Carried over: board chair #1; checklist item 6.)
*Plan, budget table:* "Fiscal sponsor fee (6% of funds raised) … Rate to be confirmed with Social Good Fund." *Legal readiness table:* "sponsorship model and dataset ownership to confirm."
*Why:* the sponsor's handbook sets 8% for Model A and 6.5% for domestic Model C, on total monthly receipts ([Social Good Fund project handbook](https://socialgoodfund.gitbook.io/project-handbook/social-good-fund-sponsorship-admin-cost)); the leadership notes of July 2026 record "Model A decided." At 8% the 2027 fee on $308K is about $24.6K, not $18.5K, and about $22K more over 2027–28 on the aggressive path. The same notes recorded that Model A was understood to sit uneasily with a separately incorporated nonprofit; CEI incorporated anyway. Three months on, a funder-facing plan still says "to confirm."
*Fix:* budget 8% until the executed agreement says otherwise, and write the promised legal-structure paragraph now: which entity directors govern, who controls funds, who employs staff, who owns data and code, the fee, the exit terms, where the $17K sits.

**M6. The 2029 move to an independent 501(c)(3) is modeled as a free, same-day switch.**
*Workbook, Assumptions H5:* "Ends when CEI holds its own 501(c)(3), assumed from 2029." *Legal line H59:* "Includes the 501(c)(3) application in 2028" ($6K). Both paths show a 0% fee from January 2029.
*Why:* Form 1023 determinations commonly take three to nine months ([Harbor Compliance](https://www.harborcompliance.com/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-501c3-status); check the IRS "Where's My Application" page); state exemption follows the federal letter; grants made to the sponsor need each funder's consent to transfer; staff must be re-hired by CEI with new payroll, benefits and insurance; and the sponsor's transfer terms are not in the documents. The multi-year awards the five-year paths depend on would be in the sponsor's name at the moment of exit.
*Fix:* model a mid-2029 transition: fee through the transfer month, a one-time $15K–$25K line (legal, duplicate insurance, payroll setup, asset and grant transfers), and one sentence: "We file Form 1023 in [quarter] 2028 and plan a mid-2029 transfer, with the sponsor's fee running until funds, staff and data move; the sponsor's exit terms are [summary, to verify]."

**M7. "No spending" for AI Governance and the Scholar Group is not true, and both charters promise money the budget does not hold.** (Carried over in part: faith funder #1, AI-safety funder #9, lab researcher P2.)
*Plan, "Three programs":* "neither adds spending in Tranche 1"; AI Governance "Continues with its volunteer team" with "human validation planned for 2027" and a "full paper in preparation for AIES or NeurIPS 2027"; Scholar Group "outreach begins Q1 2027" to about 650 prospects. *Methods Committee Charter §8:* "a budget line for members' honoraria (amount for board decision)": no such line exists in v3. *Tradition Partner Charter §4:* "the 2027 plan budgets $20K–$40K per module"; *plan budget:* "$40K" for "at least three scholars rate each gold item" across four benchmarks plus say–do, which is $8K–$10K a module.
*Why:* validating about 2,700 AI-coded statements, presenting a 2027 paper, hosting the database and contacting 650 scholars cost money or volunteer hours that no one is named to give. A tradition partner reads the charter, then the budget, and sees the promise cut by three-quarters.
*Fix:* either raise the annotation line for the modules actually built in 2027 (two at $30K, per checklist item 10's fallback) or amend the charter to "about $10K per v1 module in 2027 and $30K per module from 2028." Add a $3K methods-committee honoraria line in Tranche 1. Replace "neither adds spending" with: "Both run on volunteer time in 2027; AI Governance carries about $5K for coding runs and conference travel, and Scholar Group outreach is led by [name] at [N] hours a week."

**M8. Nobody's hours are written down, and 40 volunteers have no coordinator.** (Carried over: every Oct 27 review; checklist items 1 and 13.)
*Plan, "Team and governance":* "the president serves as unpaid interim leader, reporting monthly to the board (proposed; hours to confirm). Benchmark v1 is led by [name], [N] hours a week." *Operating rhythm:* weekly, monthly and quarterly outputs with no producer named until an ED exists. *Risk table, row 1:* every founder-capacity mitigation is a hire that depends on money.
*Why:* the president is a company's chief scientific officer and the research director is leaving his academic post. Volunteer agreements are unsigned, the recusal rule needs employers confirmed, and no one leads AI Governance or the Scholar Group by name. Volunteer organizations lose founders not in a crisis but in the fourth month of unowned monthly reports.
*Fix:* a "who does what until the first hire" table (role, person, hours a week, through what date); a named volunteer operations lead from November 2026; the chair's quarterly check of founder hours against that table; agreements signed before the plan circulates. If the hours cannot be written, remove the 12-month date.

## 3. Should fix

**S1. "Conservative" is a sixfold jump.** *Five-year table:* "Conservative: revenue | $100K | $600K." No research nonprofit grows sixfold in a year without a multi-year award already in hand, and none is in the pipeline. Rename the paths "Base" and "Upside," or recut 2028 base to $250K–$350K, and add: "2028 on both paths depends on one or two multi-year awards closing in 2027."

**S2. The ED job is three jobs.** The posting draft and the plan give the operating leader operations, fundraising across four channels, partnerships, board formation, the sponsorship exit and 40 volunteers, at 24 hours a week. Scope 2027 to three dated deliverables (board seated and compliant; $[X] received; sponsor, legal and volunteer agreements complete) and give research management to the v1 lead.

**S3. "Half-time" engineer, ten hours a week.** (Carried over: board chair #6, now a footnote.) All four documents still say "half-time" in the body; the footnote says "roughly 500 contract hours … closer to a quarter." Write "a part-time contract engineer (about 10 hours a week)" everywhere, or raise the line.

**S4. A donor can still own the leader.** (Carried over: board chair #9.) The gift-policy fixes were adopted except "No donor is named sponsor of a staff role," yet the invitation table and Christian edition still sell "the Executive Director's first year." Reword as "general operating support that lets us hire our operating leader."

**S5. No give/get range.** (Carried over.) *Plan:* "every board member gives personally at a level meaningful to them." The $50K board and major-donor line needs a number a candidate can picture: "a personal gift or secured gifts of $[5K–$25K, to decide] in the first year."

**S6. The operating rhythm has no cash line and no owner.** Add "Monthly: cash received against the three tiers, sponsor statement reconciled, pipeline updated (owner: treasurer until the operating lead starts)," and put tier status at the top of each quarterly report. "Financials reviewed by an outside accountant" is a $5K–$10K engagement (to verify) absent from the $5K accounting line, and may duplicate the sponsor's audit.

**S7. State compliance reads as "not done."** *Legal readiness table:* "Tax ID, bank account, insurance, accounting | To confirm." A California nonprofit corporation must have a secretary and a treasurer or CFO now, not "at the first meeting" ([Corp. Code §5213](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_corp_code_section_5213)), and owes a Statement of Information, Attorney General registration if it holds assets, and a state exemption filing to avoid the minimum franchise tax (to verify each with counsel). Replace "to confirm" with a dated table of filings and owners, including a date and quote for the D&O policy.

**S8. The IRB route depends on a partner that does not yet exist.** *Workbook H60:* "University partner or an independent board," $3K on the aggressive path and $0 on the conservative one, while gold-set ratification falls in months 4–8 and the hosted fellow arrives only with Tranche 2. Budget an independent-board determination in Tranche 1 (about $1K–$3K, to verify) and name the route.

## 4. Nits

- *Budget basis:* "2026 California small-nonprofit ED benchmarks ($50K–$70K at 50–60% time)." The cited brief's benchmark is $85K–$115K full time; say "pro-rated from."
- Board brief "a 45-minute conversation" vs plan "a 30-minute conversation."
- "Tranche 1 alone … funds 12 months of operations" assumes all $202K arrives up front; say "once received."
- Workbook "Paid people at year end" counts the hosted fellow, the university's employee; say "3 paid by CEI plus 1 hosted."
- Technical brief "2027 hires" omits the technical co-lead stipend the budget carries.
- Methods Committee Charter §8 "CEI provides staff support": say who, in 2027.

## 5. What is strong

- The three dated tiers with the March 31 trigger and "commit spending only against money in hand." Few founders write their own floor; keep it and lead with it.
- Founder compensation at $0 as a visible line, and an ED pay path requiring a comparables file and disinterested approval at each step.
- The workbook: every total a formula off blue inputs, unit costs on one sheet, a change log naming who decided what and when.
- The independence policy with numbers and the two charters with teeth: partner veto over method changes, withdrawal on notice, dissent published unedited, no funder review.
- "Still open, stated plainly" in the board brief. It buys more trust than any traction claim; keep it current.
