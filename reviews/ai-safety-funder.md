# Review: AI-safety technical grantmaker

*Adversarial read of the Oct 26, 2026 business plan and technical brief, with the theory-of-change evidence report and methods/risks note as context, by a program officer at a technical AI-safety funder deciding on a ~$300K ask.*

## 1. Verdict

**Fund a smaller, scoped pilot; pass on the institutional ask for now.** The only parts a safety funder should pay for are the say–do module and the identity-cue sycophancy probe: they are the capability-decorrelated, behavioral measurements, and they map onto failures labs already treat as costly. The four knowledge-style benchmarks are, by the plan's own evidence, likely to track capability like ETHICS, enter a crowded field, and have no precedent of changing lab behavior. The team cannot execute the stated scope: the accountable v1 lead is a literal placeholder, there is no technical co-lead, no pilot result, and $40K of annotation must cover four gold sets plus a fifth module. I would offer roughly $75–100K [my proposal, not in the plan] for a pre-registered say–do plus sycophancy pilot on two modules, conditional on a named technical co-lead with published evaluation work, and revisit the rest once there is a transcript to look at.

## 2. Top problems, ranked

**1. The say–do module carries the whole safety case but is undersized and under-specified. [JIMMY]**
Brief, "Say–do": *"consistency (action vs. the model's own judgment on the abstracted case)"*; *"about 200 scenarios estimate each model's overall gap to within about ±7 percentage points."* Three design gaps. (a) The "say" side is an explicit moral question while the "do" side hides the stakes, so a gap could be perception failure (the model never noticed the stakes) rather than akrasia; nothing distinguishes them. Add a salience arm (stakes implicit vs. explicit) and a post-hoc "what did you notice?" probe. (b) Fidelity to a tradition in an agentic scenario needs the model to know which tradition applies; the brief never says whether a tradition cue is in the system prompt. Without it, fidelity is undefined; with it, the task becomes steerability-in-action, which is fine but must be stated. Specify a 2×2: tradition cue present/absent × conflict present/absent. (c) ±7pp at n=200 means two models must differ by >10pp to be distinguished; six models will mostly be "indistinguishable." Grow this module (it is the product) or stop promising per-model comparisons. And since "say" is itself prompt-unstable (Gu et al., in your own note), report test–retest stability of the "say" measure before attributing any gap.

**2. The accountable lead is blank and the first hire is the wrong one for this funder. [JIMMY]**
Plan, Team: *"Benchmark v1 is led by [name], [N] hours a week."* A safety funder reads that sentence and stops. Tranche 1 buys a 60% ED and a 50% engineer and zero senior research time, while lab-affiliated researchers (Jakkli, Rajamanoharan, Nanda) already produce spec-grounded say–do audits with automated agents. Restructure: a full-time evals lead (or a named technical co-lead at ≥20 hrs/week) is the Tranche 1 hire for safety money; faith funders fund the ED. State that split in "Matching the ask to the funder."

**3. Evaluation awareness is handled by reading transcripts, which is the weakest available method. [TEXT]**
Brief, threats table: *"transcripts reviewed for evaluation awareness, with its rate reported."* Models increasingly do not verbalize test-awareness, and reasoning traces may be summarized or unavailable via API [verify per vendor]. Transcript review gives a lower bound. Add: a matched "believed-real" arm (deployment-style framing with real tool side-effects in a sandbox), a direct post-hoc question, and report the gap conditional on stated belief, as Anthropic did (55.1% vs. 6.5%). Replace with: "We report say–do gaps split by the model's stated belief about whether the scenario was real, and treat transcript-only detection as a lower bound."

**4. Capability-correlation on six models is not a check; and the encyclical module may measure training cutoff. [TEXT]**
Brief: *"We report each dimension's correlation with standard capability benchmarks across the evaluated models"* with *"At least six frontier and open-weight models."* A correlation on n=6 can neither confirm nor refute Safetywashing-style confounding; Ren et al. used dozens of models. Fix: run the cheap text tasks on 20–30 open-weight models spanning sizes (local, near-free) for the correlation analysis, and say so. Separately, *Magnifica humanitas* was published May 2026; models with earlier cutoffs cannot "represent" it. Representation scores will partly rank models by cutoff date. Add an open-book arm (source text in context) and report closed-book vs. open-book side by side.

**5. The safety contribution is misdescribed in one direction and underclaimed in the other. [TEXT]**
Brief, Summary: *"independent public checks of whether models' value specifications are coherent across framings."* No task takes a lab's spec or constitution as input; CEI tests tradition representation, not spec coherence. Either add a small spec-grounded arm (reuse Jakkli et al.'s tenets) or delete the sentence. *"so any community can check any model without a lab's permission"* is true only of open-weight models; frontier models are API-only. Write "any open-weight model." Meanwhile the strongest lab-actionable finding, sycophancy toward stated religious identity, gets one row and no budget share. Lead the safety pitch with sycophancy and say–do, with a stated share of Tranche 1.

**6. Harness-first distribution contradicts the private split, and nobody pays for or owns the split. [TEXT] + [JIMMY]**
Plan, Pathway 5: *"Tests released in standard evaluation harnesses (Inspect, lm-evaluation-harness)"* versus Measurement design: *"a private test split run through an evaluation server."* Inspect Evals is a public repo; only the dev split can live there, so what labs can run in an afternoon is not what CEI reports. Decide: an Inspect task that calls CEI's server for held-out items, or accept the dev split as the product. Then the money: no budget line funds the server's ongoing cost, GPU hosting for open-weight models, or the 6-month item rotation (fresh scholar-authored items at your own $20–40K per module). "Named custodians" are unnamed; zero-data-retention terms from every vendor cannot be assumed by a fiscally sponsored nonprofit [verify]; and dataset ownership sits with the fiscal sponsor under an unresolved model (OPEN-QUESTIONS §1). Who holds the keys if CEI dissolves?

**7. The theory of change's causal clause is contradicted by the plan's own evidence, and the accountability framing now removes the outcome. [TEXT]**
Plan, ToC: *"because rigorous, endorsed and easy-to-run work is what both communities trust and use"*; the methods note says *"I found no study that causally links peer review to adoption"* and that the benchmarks labs report are capability ones. Then Measuring impact: *"our success does not depend on any lab's decision."* Together, this asks to be funded for outputs with no falsifiable use criterion. Rewrite the clause: "because evaluations that are cheap, automated, paired-prompt, held-out and already in the harnesses safety institutes use are the ones that get run; peer review earns trust, not adoption." Keep publishing as the accountability bar, but add a decision rule: "If no party outside CEI has run the suite by month 18, the board does not fund v2 on the same distribution model."

**8. The assumptions table tests the wrong things in 12 months. [TEXT]**
Plan, Key assumptions: Validity is tested by *"say–do correlation"*, which is circular (a low correlation is either unstable dispositions or a real gap). Demand is tested by *"a pilot with one evaluation organization"* that the partner table lists under *"Partners we still need."* Credibility is tested at month 24. Missing entirely is discriminant validity: do the four modules' profiles differ from each other and from a generic ethics score? If module profiles inter-correlate at 0.9+, the pluralism premise adds nothing to measurement. That is testable in 12 months and should be assumption one. Replace the Validity test with "cross-format stability of each measure, reported separately from the say–do gap; between-module correlation reported."

**9. The validation plan does not fit the budget or the calendar. [JIMMY]**
Plan: *"Expert annotation and tradition leads (stipends and gold-set rating) — $40K"* against four modules × 300–500 items × ≥3 raters (3,600–6,000 ratings), say–do gold, a fifth module, and instrument re-validation: under $10 per rating, nothing left for stipends, and all in an unfunded Tranche 2. Cut v1 to two modules (*Magnifica humanitas*, where a defined magisterium makes gold sets tractable, plus one secular module) and the two behavioral tasks. Check the venue arithmetic too: 12 months from a Q1 2027 tranche lands in Q1 2028, after the ICLR 2028 deadline [verify]; the honest target is NeurIPS 2028 Datasets & Benchmarks, with an earlier preprint.

**10. The moral-psychology module is a research program disguised as a benchmark. [JIMMY]**
Brief: *"Moral Foundations, Schwartz and Kohlberg-style instruments re-validated on models."* Your own note shows survey-style answers collapse toward random under order randomization, the DIT critique is unanswered, and Kohlberg's stage theory is contested for humans [verify]. No fidelity score applies. Drop it from v1 or label it exploratory with no profile claims.

**11. "No leaderboard" is a publication preference, not a property of open data. [TEXT]**
Plan: *"no ranked leaderboard, badges or certifications."* Once the dev split and code are public, anyone can rank. Say so, and make the profile report useful anyway: ship, per model, the catalog of specific misstatements against each tradition's "known errors," the say–do transcripts, and the sycophancy flips. Those artifacts are what a lab's character or behavior team can act on; a per-dimension score is not.

**12. Conflicts of interest for lab-employed volunteers are unaddressed. [TEXT]**
Plan: volunteers' *"day jobs include Google DeepMind, Meta, Scale AI, ByteDance."* Add to the data-access policy: no private-split custodian, item author or judge-prompt author employed by an evaluated lab; disclosed in the datasheet.

## 3. Keep

- **Construct separation** (describe / apply / own stance) is cleaner than nearly every values benchmark I see, and the sycophancy rule "flip rate on items where the traditions agree" neatly escapes the legitimate-flip objection.
- **Reporting commitments** are BetterBench-grade: refusals as a separate column, item-level bootstrap intervals, dev–test gap published side by side, cross-vendor judges never from the scored family, exact model versions and decoding settings.
- **Naming the closest prior art**, including the Jakkli et al. constitution audit and Agentic Misalignment, rather than hiding it.
- **Independence rules with teeth**: embargo without edit rights, revenue caps, a stated willingness to publish a poor Claude result, and a Foresight-style full-open commitment.
- **Self-undermining honesty** in the evidence section ("labs ask for dialogue, not tests"). Most proposals bury this.

## 4. Technical brief specifically

A frontier-lab evals researcher would read it to the end: it is short, has prior-work and threats tables, and does not oversell. The first pushbacks, in order: (1) "Show me one item and one say–do transcript." There is no example anywhere; a mock profile report for one open-weight model would do more than the whole brief. (2) How is "say" elicited, how many times, with what stability? (3) n=6 for a capability correlation. (4) Jakkli et al. already ran 400 tenets across seven models with an automated auditing agent; why is tradition-grounded better, and why author by hand? (5) Who builds 200 sandboxed tool-use environments on a half-time engineer? (6) "Claim-level match" is an LLM judge, and the judge thresholds are listed as an open question, so "pre-registered" is a promise, not a status; add a registry link or drop the word. (7) "Fairly represented only when X's scholars agree": at what α, and what happens when the magisterium and the moral theologians split?
