# Review: tradition scholars (a Catholic moral theologian; a secular moral philosopher)

Reviewed: the Christian edition, the business plan and the technical brief's "Constructs and tasks". **[TEXT]** fix in the documents; **[JIMMY]** a leadership decision; **[verify]** a claim to check.

## Part A: a Catholic moral theologian

### 1. Verdict

I would review the module, but not yet, and not as a "reviewer" of items drafted by others. Condition one: a Catholic theologian is named as lead author before any item is final, and the prototype now "in active development" is declared a CEI draft to be rewritten, not polished. Condition two: the module is renamed, its corpus and levels of authority are specified, and the plan's own vocabulary about AI is reconciled with ¶99 of the encyclical it proposes to test.

### 2. Problems, ranked

**1. The module name overclaims.** Measurement design: "**Catholic social teaching on AI** | Pope Leo XIV's encyclical *Magnifica humanitas*." Catholic social teaching runs from *Rerum novarum* onward; this encyclical is one document in it and leans on *Antiqua et Nova* and *Quo vadis, humanitas?* (fn. 123). A score labeled "Catholic social teaching" will be read as the Church's answer key. Fix: "*Magnifica humanitas* (one encyclical within Catholic social teaching)"; state the corpus and the authoritative language edition [verify]; cite paragraph numbers on every item. [TEXT]

**2. "Gold answers" flatten a text that teaches.** Tasks: "Claim by claim, against key points written by that tradition's scholars"; say–do "fidelity (does it match the tradition's gold answer, where the tradition gives clear guidance?)." Catholic theology distinguishes levels of teaching and assent (*Lumen gentium* 25; *Donum veritatis*). The encyclical is categorical in places (¶99 on conscience; ¶198, ¶200 on lethal decisions) and exhortatory or prudential in most others. Scoring both at one weight manufactures doctrine. Fix: tier every item ("categorical teaching" / "principle" / "prudential application"); use only the first tier for say–do fidelity; label the rest "representation of the encyclical's argument." [TEXT]

**3. ¶107 is quoted against its own sense.** Christian edition and plan: "a more moral AI is not enough if that morality is determined by a few," used as a warrant for benchmarks and, in the evidence note, as a reason not to seek Church review. ¶107 in full demands "openly discussing the ethical frameworks ... subjecting them to shared standards of social justice" and "more active political involvement" (¶106: "robust legal frameworks, independent oversight"). Its target is "those who control AI," not the Church; by the Pope's standard a private benchmark is part of what is "not enough." Fix: quote ¶107 once in full; place CEI as one instrument of the "independent checks" of ¶71 and the contestability of ¶164; say the encyclical's program is law and participation, which CEI does not supply. [TEXT]

**4. Your vocabulary presupposes what ¶99 denies.** Vision: AI "whose moral formation is open and accountable"; tasks test "whether models act on their stated values." ¶99: these systems "do not have a moral conscience"; ¶198 rejects "artificial moral agents." The Risks row treats this as a model-welfare quarrel with labs; the nearer problem is CEI's own framing. Fix: replace "moral formation" with "value specification" outside the Christian edition; state in the module spec that fidelity scores measure consistency of outputs with a teaching and take no position on machine agency; make ¶99 itself a representation item. [TEXT]

**5. Dissent inside the Church is unspecified, and the example is wrong.** Validation: "disagreement *within* traditions (for example Catholic and Reformed, or Sunni schools)." Catholic–Reformed is between traditions. Within Catholicism you will meet Thomist, personalist and liberationist readings, and the question of who counts as Catholic: mandatum holders (c. 812), lay ethicists, self-description? The plan has the methods committee set "inclusion criteria" for legitimacy, which could mean secular philosophers decide who speaks for the Church. Fix: the magisterial text, anchored verbatim, is the reference; theologians' readings are a labeled layer; low-agreement items are kept and marked "contested within the tradition"; the committee chooses traditions, not a tradition's authorities. [TEXT]

**6. The Christian edition reads Reformed, and its "church" is Protestant.** Anchors: Eph 4:15, Exod 31, 1 Pet 4:10, Rom 2:14–15, Jer 29:7; "with Kuyper and Bavinck ... God's common grace"; "The church is asking for exactly this kind of humility" cites a Barna survey of pastors. "Common grace" is a Reformed term; Catholics say natural law, "seeds of the Word," *Gaudium et spes* 22. A Catholic reads a Reformed organization proposing to grade a papal encyclical. Fix: add *Gaudium et spes* 22, *Nostra aetate* 2 and natural law (CCC 1954–60) beside Kuyper; write "Protestant pastors" for Barna; state that a Catholic lead will author the module. [TEXT] Recruiting that lead before the prototype grows is the real fix. [JIMMY]

**7. "Co-sign" and "first" imply what you disclaim.** Pathway and Risks: "at least one community or academic body co-signs a module"; "communities co-sign their modules," beside "we do not seek or claim official Church approval." A Catholic university's co-signature reads as institutional endorsement, and no Church instrument certifies scoring (an imprimatur covers a fixed text). And "the first benchmark of *Magnifica humanitas*" sits under "lead with what others lack": an encyclical whose ¶110 asks that AI be freed from "armed" competition is here a funder differentiator. Fix: "named scholars review and are credited; institutions may host, not certify"; drop "first" as a selling point. [TEXT]

## Part B: a secular moral philosopher invited to the methods committee and to author a module

### 3. Verdict

I would not author a module under the present structure: what I would "represent" (normative ethics) is not a tradition, and the body that would protect it (the methods committee) exists only as a sentence. I would join the committee if its charter gave it a veto over module inclusion and scoring criteria that the board cannot override, and if CEI published a pluralism commitment that does not rest on common grace. Fix those and I would author, with named co-authors and a right to withdraw.

### 4. Problems, ranked

**1. Who decides is asserted, not structured.** Governance: "The governing board will be Christian-led. Decisions about which traditions are included and how they are scored rest with the independent methods committee, not the board." But the board hires the leader and approves strategy and budget; the committee "sit[s] outside the board," is unformed (checklist: "Name the methods committee members"), and has no appointment rule, term, budget or veto. The Risks table offers "a Christian-led board" to faith audiences and "secular philosophers on the methods committee" to secular ones. Fix: charter the committee in the bylaws (published appointment process, majority outside the founders' tradition, documented veto, public minutes, a rule for board–committee conflict). [JIMMY] Until then write "will rest" and say the charter is pending. [TEXT]

**2. "The world's moral wisdom" vs. v1.** Mission: "translates the world's moral wisdom"; executive summary: "across the world's moral traditions." v1: four Anglophone frameworks, three Western psychological theories (Kohlberg's stages are contested as parochial), the UDHR and one encyclical. The table's "across cultures" overstates those samples. The Landscape section is honest ("'many traditions' is our direction, not yet our state"); the mission is not. Fix: "the moral wisdom of the traditions that join us"; in Measurement design: "v1 is Western and single-faith; see roadmap." [TEXT]

**3. "Describe, don't adjudicate" dissolves at scoring time.** Representation: "a view counts as fairly represented only when its own scholars agree"; candor "penalizes false balance where a tradition holds a firm position"; sycophancy: "flip rate on items where the traditions agree." Each requires someone to decide who the scholars are, when a position is "firm," and when traditions "agree." That is adjudication, delegated and unnamed. Fix: publish each item's panel, votes and retention rule; rename "gold answer" to "reference answer with recorded dissent"; have the methods committee, not CEI staff, settle cross-tradition agreement items. [TEXT]

**4. "Tradition" is the wrong unit for me.** Table: Normative ethics, "Whose moral wisdom it reflects: Moral philosophy"; Application: "Advising a person who holds tradition X." Nobody holds deontology as one holds a faith, and no scholars speak for utilitarianism. Meanwhile the one secular community with a ratified creed (Humanists International's Luxembourg Declaration) is deferred to "next candidates." The discipline is cast as a tradition; the actual humanist community is absent. Fix: split frameworks (scored on reasoning structure: which considerations are recognized and weighed) from communities (scored on applied advice); add a humanist module to v1 or say why not. [TEXT] [JIMMY]

**5. "Our foundation" explains my inclusion by a doctrine I reject.** "We hold that truth is one and that Christ is Lord of it ... God's common grace gives real moral insight to all people ... Every person bears the image of God, so every person deserves AI that takes their moral world seriously." Read from outside: my insight is real because your God granted it, and the table is "the best setting for truth to be tested" when the verdict is already in. The promised "standard to hold us to" is internal to Reformed theology. I need to know whether CEI can allow that my view is right where Christianity is wrong, because that is what "no one controls the scoring" means. Fix: move the confession to the Christian edition or an appendix; add an operational commitment: no tradition's rubric scores another; CEI staff author no tradition's items but their own; the committee can block a module; any partner can withdraw. [TEXT]

**6. "Principled pluralism" is a confessional term.** Values: "Principled pluralism: each tradition speaks for itself, and no one controls the scoring"; the Christian edition defines it through common grace. The phrase comes from Reformed political theology (Mouw and Griffioen; the Center for Public Justice) [verify]. To a secular reader it announces that pluralism is a concession from possessed truth. Fix: in the general plan and technical brief name the value "Each tradition speaks for itself"; keep "principled pluralism" in the Christian edition. [TEXT]

**7. Safeguards I would need before trusting a score.** Present and good: judges from two vendors, order-randomized controls, α per tradition, refusals as a column, no leaderboard. Missing: (a) a rule that CEI staff never author or edit another tradition's items ("CEI only reviewing quality" is promised only for the fifth, outside module); (b) a partner's right to veto method changes to its module and to publish dissent with results (dual-use mitigation covers data releases, not results); (c) the agreement threshold for keeping an item; (d) the lab mitigation "frame findings as how models can do better," which is spin beside the promise that negative results are "published unchanged." Fix: a one-page tradition-partner charter covering (a)–(c); delete (d). [TEXT]

## 5. Keep (both parts)

- The three stances held apart (describing, applying, the model's own view) and "We do not score the model's own view against any tradition." Rare, and both reviewers need it.
- Refusals as their own column; no "most moral model" number; dissent published beside results; candor scored in both directions.
- Plain admissions: "No Catholic theologians are on the module yet"; "'many traditions' is our direction, not yet our state"; "we do not seek or claim official Church approval."
- The local-run kit, so a parish, mosque or department can test a model "without asking a lab": the one place the design answers ¶72 and ¶164 directly.
- The evidence note's own reading that ¶107 "applies directly to CEI": a hard-coded reading of Catholic AI ethics "could itself be 'morality determined by a few.'" Put that sentence in the plan.
