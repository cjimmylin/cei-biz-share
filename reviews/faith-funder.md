# Faith-funder review: CEI business plan and Christian edition

*Stance: senior program officer at a faith-based foundation funding religion-and-science and faith-and-AI work. Read in full: both plans, plus the evidence report and OPEN-QUESTIONS. [TEXT] = an editor can fix now; [JIMMY] = needs a founder decision or fact.*

## 1. Verdict

First meeting, yes. Full proposal, not yet. The thesis fits this portfolio better than most of what crosses my desk: labs are consulting faith communities and nobody is turning that consultation into tests anyone can check, and the plan is unusually honest about validity. But the piece I could put before a committee, a scholar-reviewed *Magnifica humanitas* benchmark plus the say–do test, has no Catholic scholar attached, no named person accountable for shipping it, and an ask shaped as operating support for an institution rather than a research project with a lead and outputs. I can fund a module; I cannot yet fund a center.

## 2. Top problems, ranked

**1. A Reformed-founded, Protestant-networked group is building the first benchmark of a papal encyclical with no Catholics on it.** [JIMMY]
*Where:* "Our foundation" ("with Reformed theologians such as Abraham Kuyper and Herman Bavinck") against "Partners we still need" ("Catholic theologians to write and review the encyclical module").
*Why:* Catholic funders, Notre Dame, Markkula and the new USCCB task force will read a Protestant-led group grading AI on the Pope's teaching as presumptuous, and ¶107's warning about morality "determined by a few" applies to CEI's own answer key. The budget compounds it: "at least three scholars rate each item" across four modules for $40K assumes theologians who work like crowd raters. Scholars of that standing co-lead for an honorarium; they do not rate 400 items for a stipend.
*Fix:* before the module appears in any pitch, secure one named Catholic co-lead (a theologian, or a center such as Markkula, Notre Dame's Institute for Ethics and the Common Good or Georgetown's Initiative on Catholic Social Thought) at a real honorarium [amount, verify], and write: "The *Magnifica humanitas* module is co-led by [Catholic scholar or center, verify]; CEI supplies method and engineering." Until then label it "proposed, pending a Catholic co-lead" and add a Catholic seat to the board wish list.

**2. The church edition promises the church a seat that v1 does not give it.** [JIMMY]
*Where:* Christian edition opening ("whether the moral wisdom of the church ... will be at the table") and "What success looks like."
*Why:* the only Christian module in v1 is Catholic; the founders' theology and relationships are Protestant; nothing grounded in Scripture, a confession or a congregation appears anywhere in 2027–29. A pastor asks "where is my church in this?" and the honest answer is "plain-language briefs." A funder of congregational life has nothing here to fund.
*Fix:* make the "fifth, outside-authored module" Protestant or ecumenical, or say plainly: "v1 carries one Christian module, built with Catholic scholars. A Protestant module is our first v2 priority, and we are looking for the seminary or denomination to author it."

**3. Nobody named is accountable for the flagship, and both founders have other jobs.** [JIMMY]
*Where:* "Team and governance" ("Benchmark v1 is led by [name], [N] hours a week").
*Why:* a placeholder in the one sentence that answers "who does the work?" is disqualifying in a funder-facing draft. The President is a biotech CSO, the Research Director is leaving Harvard, the ED seat has turned over twice, and the plan asks faith funders to buy a 60% ED before any technical lead exists.
*Fix:* name the v1 lead and hours; state each founder's weekly commitment; consider making the research engineer and technical co-lead the first hires and the ED the second: "The product is the proof; operations follow it."

**4. The ask is shaped for the wrong funders.** [TEXT]
*Where:* "Funding model" ("Faith funders and major donors are best placed to fund the ED and the institution"); "Pending: none."
*Why:* in my experience Templeton funds research projects with a named lead and outputs, almost never a startup's ED; Lilly funds congregations and theological education, largely through intermediaries such as DELTA; Faith Work & Tech administered your DELTA grant and knows the ED history. All three are listed with no dates.
*Fix:* re-cut by funder: Templeton, a two-year research project ("Does AI act on the values it professes? A tradition-reviewed test," lead named, outputs listed); Lilly, via DELTA or with a congregational deliverable; major donors and board, the ED. Add: "If Tranche 1 is not closed by [Q2 2027, verify], v1 release moves to 2028."

**5. "One flagship" is contradicted by the 12-month target list.** [JIMMY]
*Where:* "Measuring impact," the seven 12-month rows.
*Why:* four benchmarks, say–do, a fifth outside-authored module, a local-run kit, a harness integration, a brief per benchmark, two committees, an IRB determination, two papers, a board, an ED and an engineer, on $173K–$308K. Funders who see focus claimed and then this list discount the whole timeline. One target is impossible as written: datasets released "only after testing shows they improve consistency between what models say and do," by an organization that will not train models.
*Fix:* mark the fifth module, local-run kit and per-benchmark briefs "stretch, if Tranche 2 closes by [date]," or define v1 as two benchmarks (one secular, one faith) plus say–do. For the datasets: "released after a partner lab or academic group tests whether they improve say–do consistency."

**6. The headline proof point is double-counted and unvalidated.** [TEXT]
*Where:* Executive summary ("5,140 AI-governance statements from 130 countries mapped into six recurring clusters").
*Why:* your own checklist says about 2,694 were coded and 2,646 clustered, by two AI models, with human validation "planned." A religion-and-science funder's first question is "who checked the coding?"; finding the answer nine pages later reads as spin.
*Fix:* "5,140 statements collected; about 2,650 coded so far [verify] into six clusters by two AI models, with human validation beginning Q1 2027."

**7. The plan contradicts itself about the Scholar Group.** [JIMMY]
*Where:* "Why this order" ("the Scholar Group already meets") versus "Three programs" ("About 650 prospective members identified; outreach begins Q1 2027").
*Why:* 650 names from public sources is a prospect list, not a community. Either a smaller real group meets, or "already meets" is untrue.
*Fix:* "A core of [N, verify] scholars has met [N] times since [date]; we have identified about 650 further prospects for outreach in Q1 2027."

**8. "A direct line to Chris Olah's team" is in both documents and is now as much liability as asset.** [TEXT; last sentence JIMMY]
*Where:* Executive summary, "Partners" table, Christian edition.
*Why:* Anthropic's faith outreach has been called "creepy" by a governor and ethics-washing by *Scientific American*. No faith foundation board wants to fund a lab's religious cover, and repetition reads as networking presented as traction.
*Fix:* say it once, paired with independence: "We have a working contact with Anthropic's values team and have told them we will publish results they may dislike." Consider a cleaner line for v1: no cash from any frontier lab until v1 is public.

**9. "Christian-led board" is undefined and sits in tension with the methods committee.** [JIMMY]
*Where:* "Team and governance" ("The governing board will be Christian-led. Decisions about which traditions are included ... rest with the independent methods committee").
*Why:* chair, majority, or statement of faith? And the board that approves budgets decides which modules get money, whatever the committee rules. Templeton reviewers and non-Christian partners will both probe this.
*Fix:* define the term, and add: "The methods committee's charter, adopted by the board, gives it final say on inclusion and scoring; the board commits to fund any committee-approved module within the annual budget or to state publicly why not."

**10. Relationship language runs ahead of the record.** [TEXT]
*Where:* "Partners" ("working relationships with Brian Patrick Green ... Charles Camosy and AI and Faith"; Harvard "co-authors"); "Academic work" ("a paper on epiplexity accepted at two conferences"); Christian edition ("Notre Dame and Harvard scholars co-author and review our work").
*Why:* leadership's own answer is that CEI "knows" these people; the Harvard tie behind "Harvard co-authors" ended in 2025 [verify who the Harvard co-author is]; no Notre Dame link is public; "epiplexity" is unexplained and its venues are abstract-level. A program officer makes one phone call.
*Fix:* "Introductions through Brian Patrick Green and AI and Faith; Governance Symphony co-authors at Notre Dame [names, with consent] and a former Harvard visiting scholar." Give the epiplexity paper a title, authors and venue or drop it. Name no one as "in discussion" without written consent.

## 3. Keep

- The one-sentence if/then/because theory of change and "What we are accountable for": publishing, not lab adoption. The most honest accountability statement I have seen from a startup in this space.
- "What the evidence says so far," including the findings against you (no lab uses an outside pluralism benchmark; ETHICS tracks capability). Keep it dated.
- "Principled pluralism, not relativism." The common-grace and natural-law pairing is the right grounding for a Christian organization building a plural table, and it reads well to Reformed, evangelical and Catholic eyes alike.
- Governance Symphony's finding that solidarity and religious-pastoral clusters appear only when faith, labor and Indigenous sources are included. It is your best one-line evidence that whose voice is in the data changes the result; promote it to the executive summary.

## 4. Christian edition specifically

It lands in parts. The "third way" framing (market, regulation, moral traditions) and the Barna statistic will land with pastors, and the theology is sound for Reformed, evangelical and Catholic readers. It does not patronize. It preaches to the choir, which is fine, but the choir will not read past the first screen, because the first screen is a mission statement.

- **Reorder.** Open with "The challenge in Silicon Valley," then the bridge, then the invitation; move mission, vision and values to the end. Remove the "@Jimmy" byline. [TEXT]
- **"Christians are largely absent from the technical work"** is now wrong on your own evidence (Gloo's Christian benchmark, AllFaith, Notre Dame's $50.8M, Anthropic's Christian summit). Replace: "Christians are now being consulted. Almost none of that input becomes a test anyone can check." Same fix in the general plan's "For the church: why now." [TEXT]
- **Overpromise:** "a church, seminary or school will be able to check an AI system against its own convictions." Only open-weight models, only modules that exist (none Protestant in v1), only with technical help. Write: "a seminary or Christian college with a technical partner will be able to run our tests on open models." [TEXT]
- **No people.** Jimmy and Rui appear only in the sign-off, and church donors fund people. Add two-sentence bios: MD/PhD, theology MA, Rare Genomics founder and 2012 Praxis venture; Rui's vantage on how nations govern AI. [TEXT]
- **No story.** One vignette beats any statistic: a congregant asking an AI about grief, a teenager asking about self-harm (topics the Anthropic summit reportedly discussed [verify]). OpenAI reports more than 70 million faith-related requests a week [verify]; that line makes a pastor sit up. [TEXT]
- **"Give"** names an ED, an engineer and compute, which is not what a church donor funds. Add a ladder matching the general plan: "$[5K] funds [X]; $20K–$40K sponsors a tradition's reviewers; $60K underwrites the Executive Director." [TEXT]
- **Name the Catholic/Protestant dynamic** in one sentence: "Our founders are Protestant; the first faith module is Catholic, built with Catholic scholars, because the encyclical is the most substantial Christian statement on AI yet written. A Protestant module is next." [JIMMY, depends on problem 2]
- **"Board seated: a Christian-led founding board"** is not success to a donor. Replace with: "A published benchmark the church can read, and a report on how today's models represent Christian teaching." [TEXT]
